\begin{keynote}
\textbf{What the beam responds to.} The beam does not respond to the integrated rms of the field error. Its centroid is a driven, damped oscillator (\cref{sec:robinson-picture}): it follows phase and amplitude errors below its coherent synchrotron frequency \(f_\text{coh}\) (8.8 kHz at 500 mA), amplifies those near \(f_\text{coh}\) by up to \(Q\,(f_s/f_\text{coh})^2\), about 33 today, and ignores those far above. A field specification that protects the beam is therefore a limit on the spectral density of the error near \(f_\text{coh}\) and on the line harmonics below it, not an rms value alone (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}). On 8 October 2026 the beam's phase moved by \(0.39\)--\(0.58^\circ\) rms near \(f_\text{coh}\), most of it driven by two HVPS harmonics beside the line (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).
\end{keynote}

\textbf{Key architectural features:}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Single klystron, four cavities}: One I/Q modulator drives all four cavities simultaneously. Field regulation uses the vector sum of all four cavity probes.
\item
  \textbf{Bandwidth hierarchy}: direct loop (\(\approx 70\) kHz) \(\gg\) ripple loop (lines \(\approx 1\) Hz wide at 60 Hz--2.9 kHz) \(\gg\) EPICS loops (HVPS, DAC and gain tracking \(\lesssim 0.1\) Hz; tuners 0.01--0.05 Hz).

\subsubsection{Cavity Transfer Function}\label{sec:cavity-tf}


The cavity responds to the RF envelope (the baseband I/Q signal) of the current that drives it. For a cavity detuned by \(\Delta\omega = \omega_0 - \omega_\text{RF}\), the envelope transfer function from driving current to gap voltage is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cav-tf}
H_\text{cav}(s) = \frac{R_L \,\omega_{1/2}}{s + \omega_{1/2} - j\Delta\omega},
\qquad R_L = \frac{R_s}{1+\beta},
\end{equation}
where \(s\) is the Laplace variable of the envelope (\(s = j2\pi f\) at an offset \(f\) from the carrier) and \(\omega_{1/2} = \omega_0/(2Q_L)\) is the loaded half-bandwidth; the response peaks at the cavity's own resonance, \(f = \Delta\omega/2\pi\). The \textbf{impedance seen by the beam} at an absolute frequency \(\omega\) near \(\omega_0\) is the same resonance written in the RF domain:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cav-imp}
Z_\text{cav}(\omega) = \frac{R_L}{1 + j\,2Q_L\,(\omega - \omega_0)/\omega_0}
\end{equation}

Both carry the \emph{loaded} shunt impedance \(R_L = \qty{0.78}{\mega\ohm}\): the coupler and the matched circulator load appear in parallel with the cavity wall losses.

\textbf{Cavity half-bandwidth:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cav-bw}
\Delta f_{1/2} = \frac{f_0}{2Q_L} = \frac{476.3\;\text{MHz}}{2 \times 6700} = 35.5\;\text{kHz}
\end{equation}

This determines the cavity natural response time \(\tau_\text{cav} = 1/(2\pi\Delta f_{1/2}) \approx 4.5\;\mu\text{s}\).

\subsubsection{Beam Loading --- Steady-State Phasor Analysis}\label{sec:beam-loading}


A bunched beam of DC current \(I_b\) presents a fundamental RF current \(I_{b1} = 2I_b\) to the cavity, so at 500 mA the cavity sees \qty{1.0}{\ampere} at the RF frequency. Its steady-state induced voltage in the \emph{loaded} cavity, on resonance, is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vb-res}
V_{b,\text{res}} = I_{b1} R_L = \frac{I_{b1} R_s}{1+\beta} = \frac{1.0\;\text{A} \times 3.73\;\text{M}\Omega}{4.78} = 0.78\;\text{MV}
\end{equation}

and the beam draws twice as much power as the cavity walls dissipate (\crefrange{eq:pwall}{eq:pbeam}). \textbf{Beam loading, not wall loss, is what sets the design of this station.}

\textbf{Synchronous phase} --- In the convention where \(\phi_s\) is measured from the voltage crest (SLAC convention, used throughout this document):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:phis}
V_\text{RF} \cos\phi_s = U_0
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:phis-eval}
\cos\phi_s = \frac{U_0}{V_\text{RF}} = \frac{1.09\;\text{MeV}}{2.85\;\text{MV}} = 0.382 \implies \phi_s \approx 67.5^\circ
\end{equation}

Note: \(\sin\phi_s = \sin(67.5^\circ) = 0.924\), which appears in the beam loading compensation formulas below.

\textbf{Synchrotron frequency}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:nus}
\nu_s = \sqrt{\frac{h\,\alpha_c\,V_\text{RF}\,\sin\phi_s}{2\pi\,E_0}}
\end{equation}

\begin{keynote}
With \(h = 372\), \(V_\text{RF} = 2.85\) MV, \(\sin\phi_s = 0.924\) and \(E_0 = 3000\) MeV: \(\nu_s \approx 0.0078\), giving \(f_s = \nu_s \, f_\text{rev} \approx 10.0\) kHz. Published values: \(Q_s = 0.008\) \R{1}, \(\nu_s = 0.007\) \R{3} --- the range reflects differences in operational \(V_\text{RF}\) and \(\alpha_c\) across lattice configurations. For control design, the key fact is that \(f_s\) lies well inside the cavity half-bandwidth (\qty{35.5}{\kilo\hertz}), so the cavity impedance at the two synchrotron sidebands, together with the direct loop, sets the damping of the dipole synchrotron mode (\cref{sec:robinson}).
\end{keynote}

\textbf{Optimum detuning} --- The cavity is detuned so that its susceptance cancels the reactive part of the beam-induced current, leaving the generator driving a real load. Writing \(G_c = 1/R_s\) for the cavity conductance (so that \(P_\text{wall} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_\text{gap}^2 G_c\), consistent with \cref{eq:pwall}), the reactive balance \(V_\text{gap}\,G_c\tan\psi_0 + I_{b1}\sin\phi_s = 0\) gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-opt}
\tan\psi_{0,\text{opt}} = -\frac{I_{b1}\,R_s\,\sin\phi_s}{V_\text{gap}}
  = -\frac{1.0 \times 3.73 \times 10^{6} \times 0.924}{712 \times 10^{3}} = -4.84
\end{equation}

Here \(\psi_0\) is referred to the \emph{unloaded} bandwidth. The angle the tuner loop actually measures --- the phase between the cavity probe and the forward wave --- is referred to the \emph{loaded} bandwidth, and the two are related by \(\tan\psi_L = \tan\psi_0/(1+\beta)\):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-opt-eval}
\tan\psi_{L,\text{opt}} = \frac{-4.84}{1 + 3.78} = -1.01
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\psi_{L,\text{opt}} \approx -45.4^\circ
\end{equation}

\hl{The optimum detuning is proportional to the beam current}, so the tuner setpoint, the coherent synchrotron frequency and the best direct-loop setting all change with current (\cref{sec:transient-detuning,sec:robinson}). 

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Derivation note}: \Cref{eq:psi-opt} is the condition that the reactive component of the generator current vanish, which minimizes the power reflected at the input coupler. It contains the fundamental current \(I_{b1} = 2I_b\), not \(I_b\). Written with the geometry factor \(R/Q = R_s/Q_0 = \qty{116.6}{\ohm}\), as in \cref{eq:df-opt}, it no longer depends on \(Q\).
\end{keynote}

\textbf{Optimum frequency detuning} --- converting the unloaded angle to a frequency offset with \(\tan\psi_0 = 2Q_0\Delta f/f_0\):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:df-opt}
\begin{split}
\Delta f_\text{opt} &= \frac{f_0}{2Q_0}\tan\psi_{0,\text{opt}}
  = -\frac{(R/Q)\,I_{b1}\,\sin\phi_s\,f_0}{2\,V_\text{gap}} \\
  &= -\frac{116.6\;\Omega \times 1.0\;\text{A} \times 0.924 \times 476.3\;\text{MHz}}{2 \times 712\;\text{kV}}
  \approx -36.0\;\text{kHz}
\end{split}
\end{equation}

The cavity must therefore be tuned about \qty{36}{\kilo\hertz} \textbf{below} \(f_\text{RF}\) at 500 mA --- roughly one cavity half-bandwidth \eqref{eq:cav-bw}, which is why the detuning angle comes out near \(-45^\circ\). \Cref{fig:beam-loading-phasor} shows the resulting steady-state phasor balance; \cref{sec:transient} follows what happens when the beam current or \(f_\text{RF}\) moves away from this operating point.

\textbf{Required generator power per cavity} --- At optimum coupling (\(\beta_\text{opt}\)) and optimum detuning (\(\psi_\text{opt}\)), the minimum generator power per cavity is the sum of cavity wall losses and beam power:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pgen}
P_\text{gen/cav} = P_\text{wall} + \frac{P_\text{beam}}{n_\text{cav}} = \frac{V_\text{gap}^2}{2R_s} + \frac{I_b \, U_0}{n_\text{cav}}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Evaluation:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pwall}
P_\text{wall} = \frac{V_\text{gap}^2}{2R_s} = \frac{(712\;\text{kV})^2}{2 \times 3.73\;\text{M}\Omega} = 68\;\text{kW/cavity}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pbeam}
\frac{P_\text{beam}}{n_\text{cav}} = \frac{I_b \, U_0}{n_\text{cav}} = \frac{0.5\;\text{A} \times 1.09\;\text{MeV}}{4} = 136.3\;\text{kW/cavity}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pgen-eval}
P_\text{gen/cav} = 68 + 136.3 \approx 204\;\text{kW/cavity}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Total RF power}: \(4 \times 204.3 \approx 817\) kW --- within the 1.5 MW klystron capacity, leaving \(\sim 46\%\) headroom. The measured forward power on 7 October 2026 (499 mA) was 193--214 kW per cavity, \qty{829}{\kilo\watt} in total, consistent with this calculation.

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Note}: \cref{eq:pgen} is the minimum-power condition at optimum coupling. In the general case with arbitrary coupling \(\beta\) and detuning \(\psi\), the generator power includes reflected power terms. The important identity is that beam power per cavity \(= I_b V_\text{gap}\cos\phi_s = I_b \, U_0/n_\text{cav}\).
\end{keynote}

\textbf{Saturation model:} 

At fixed operating conditions, the static klystron power-transfer characteristic is approximated by 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kly-sat} 
  P_{\mathrm{out}} = P_{\infty} \frac{P_{\mathrm{in}}/P_{1/2}} {1 + P_{\mathrm{in}}/P_{1/2}}, 
\end{equation} 
where $P_{\infty}$ is the asymptotic output-power parameter and $P_{1/2}$ is the input power at which $P_{\mathrm{out}}=P_{\infty}/2$, not the input power at saturation. The small-signal power gain is $G_0=P_{\infty}/P_{1/2}$. This phenomenological model requires validation against measured power-transfer data and does not describe AM/PM conversion.

\textbf{AM-PM conversion} --- klystron output phase sensitivity to cathode voltage \R{13}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kly-ampm}

\subsubsection{SCR Phase Control Dynamics}\label{sec:hvps-scr}


\begin{equation}\label{eq:scr-cos}
V_\text{dc} = V_\text{dc,max} \cos\alpha
\end{equation}

The installed firing electronics is an Enerpro FCOG6100 six-pulse board with an FCOAUX60 auxiliary gate-drive board \R{20}; the FCOG1200 is the \emph{replacement} part and is not in service. The board's phase-locked loop free-runs at \(384 \times f_\text{line} = 23.04\) kHz and locks to the AC mains zero crossings, so the delay angle \(\alpha\) is referenced to the line rather than to an internal ramp.

For small-signal control analysis the firing chain is modeled as a single-pole lag:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:enerpro}
H_\text{Enerpro}(s) = \frac{1}{1 + s/\omega_\text{Enerpro}}
\end{equation}
with \(\omega_\text{Enerpro} \approx 415\) rad/s, i.e.\ \(-3\) dB at 66 Hz. That figure comes from the published loop-filter analysis of this firing-circuit family \R{19}, which also gives unity DC gain, a \(+0.81\) dB peak at 60 rad/s and \(-45^\circ\) phase margin at 339 rad/s. The single pole is therefore a simplification: the response is mildly underdamped rather than exactly first order.

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Small-signal bandwidth and step settling are different numbers.} The 415 rad/s pole corresponds to \(\tau = 1/\omega_\text{Enerpro} = 2.4\) ms, while a \emph{step} in the SIG HI command settles in \textbf{2--3 AC line cycles}, 33--50 ms \R{19}. The firing interval does not explain the difference. The 12-pulse rectifier fires 12 times per line cycle, once every \(1/(12 f_\text{line}) = 1.39\) ms, and a new delay angle takes effect at the next firing, so sampling delays a change by at most 1.39 ms; 2--3 cycles are 24--36 firings. Both numbers belong to the phase-locked loop that times the firings: SIG HI is summed into the PLL's loop filter, so \(\alpha\) follows a command through the closed PLL --- a small change with the 415 rad/s pole, a large step settling completely only after 2--3 cycles.
\end{keynote}

\subsubsection{PLC Voltage Regulation Loop}\label{sec:hvps-plc}


The SLC-500 PLC implements a first-order Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) filter with scan period \(T = 10\) ms:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:plc-iir}
y[m] = (1-\lambda)\,y[m-1] + \lambda\,x[m]
\end{equation}
with \(\lambda = 0.4\), giving time constant \(\tau = -T/\ln(1-\lambda) \approx 20\) ms \R{21}.

\subsubsection{Klystron Arc Protection}\label{sec:hvps-arc}


Four mechanisms limit the energy an arc can deposit in the klystron: the SCR crowbar, the star-point bypass, the \(500\;\Omega\) filter isolation resistors and the cable termination inductors. The published PEP-II result is \(< 5\) J to the klystron with the crowbar operating and \(I^2 t < 40\;\text{A}^2\text{s}\) \R{17}.

\subsection{Loop Delay Budget --- The Fundamental Bandwidth Ceiling}\label{sec:delay-budget}

The total delay around the direct loop limits the usable proportional gain. For an on-resonance, single-pole cavity, the open-loop response is \begin{equation} L(f) = \frac{G}{1 + j f/\Delta f_{1/2}}\, e^{-j 2\pi f\tau_d}, \end{equation} where \(G\) is the low-frequency loop gain. The unity-gain condition gives \(f_c = \Delta f_{1/2}\sqrt{G^2-1} \approx G\,\Delta f_{1/2}\) for \(G \gg 1\). At frequencies well above the cavity half-bandwidth, the cavity phase approaches \(-90^\circ\), while the delay adds a phase lag of \(2\pi f\tau_d\) radians. A phase margin of at least \(45^\circ\) therefore requires, approximately, \begin{equation} 2\pi f_c\tau_d \le \frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad G \lesssim \frac{1}{8\tau_d\,\Delta f_{1/2}}. \end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fc-max}
\boxed{f_{c,\text{max}} \approx \frac{1}{8\tau_d},\qquad
G_\text{max} \approx \frac{\pi}{4\,\omega_{1/2}\,\tau_d}}
\end{equation}


The second form is Gamp's maximum-gain rule \R{9}. It bounds the impedance reduction that any direct loop can provide. The often-quoted \(1/(4\tau_d)\) is the frequency at which the delay alone has consumed \(90^\circ\); a crossover there needs a lead network to supply the phase margin. \Cref{tab:robinson-delay} checks \cref{eq:fc-max} against the full loop model.

\section{I/Q Signal Processing and the LLRF Signal Chain}\label{sec:iq}


\subsection{Baseband I/Q Representation}\label{sec:iq-baseband}


All RF feedback loops act on the complex envelope of the RF signals, written in in-phase and quadrature (I/Q) components:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:iq-def}
V_\text{RF}(t) =A(t)\cos[\omega_\text{RF}t + \phi(t)] = I(t)\cos(\omega_\text{RF}t) - Q(t)\sin(\omega_\text{RF}t)
\end{equation}
where \(I(t) = A(t)\cos\phi(t)\) and \(Q(t) = A(t)\sin\phi(t)\), with inverse relations:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:iq-aphi}
A(t) = \sqrt{I^2 + Q^2}\,,\qquad \phi(t) = \text{atan2}(Q, I)
\end{equation}

A gain change scales the I/Q vector and a phase shift rotates it, so every operation in the loop --- weighting a cavity probe, setting the loop gain and phase, comparing with the reference --- is a real \(2\times2\) matrix acting on \((I, Q)\). Every loop transfer function in this document is written for the complex envelope.

\subsection{The LLRF Signal Chain}\label{sec:iq-chain}

\Cref{fig:llrf-chain} follows the signals through the legacy RF processor (RFP), an analog VXI module of PEP-II design \R{13}, \R{36}; the LLRF9 performs the same operations digitally. In order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the four cavity probe signals are demodulated to I/Q (\cref{sec:iq-demod});
\item a combining network weights each probe and forms the vector sum (\cref{sec:iq-vectorsum});
\item the direct-loop modulator sets the loop gain and phase on the vector sum, and can rotate the phase with the mean cavity detuning (frequency-offset tracking, \cref{sec:robinson-legacy});
\item difference amplifiers subtract the station reference --- a pair of RFP DACs set by the DAC loop \R{36} --- and amplify the error by 15 dB \R{13};
\item lead and integral compensation, common to I and Q, and a diode limiter shape the error;
\item an I/Q RF modulator converts it to the klystron drive \R{13}.
\end{enumerate}

The ripple DSP works alongside this chain: it compares the klystron output with the reference and applies its phase corrections, and the gain-tracking coefficient, through a further baseband modulator in the drive path (\cref{sec:loop-ripple,sec:loop-gain-tracking}).

\subsection{I/Q Demodulation}\label{sec:iq-demod}

Each probe signal is converted to baseband by a high-level (\(+13\) dBm) I/Q demodulator; the mixer outputs are low-pass filtered (\(F_c = 225\) MHz) and amplified by 17 dB to \(\pm 1\) V full scale, the input range of the multipliers that follow \R{13}.

\subsection{Cavity Probe Vector Sum}\label{sec:iq-vectorsum}

A programmable combining network --- four baseband modulators and two summing amplifiers --- weights each demodulated probe with a complex gain set by DACs, so that the sum represents the total accelerating voltage of the station \R{13}.

\subsection{Error Signal}\label{sec:iq-error}

The loop acts on the difference between the reference and the measured vector sum:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:err}
\vec{E} = \vec{V}_\text{ref} - \vec{V}_\text{sum},
\qquad
\vec{V}_\text{drive}(s) = C(s)\,\vec{E}(s),
\end{equation}
where \(C(s)\) is the controller of \cref{eq:gol}: loop gain and phase, lead and integral compensation.

\subsection{Baseband I/Q Modulator}\label{sec:iq-modulator}

Every gain and phase operation in the chain is a baseband modulator, a scaled rotation

\begin{equation}\label{eq:iq-mod}
\begin{pmatrix} I_\text{out} \\ Q_\text{out} \end{pmatrix} = G_m \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta_m & -\sin\theta_m \\ \sin\theta_m & \cos\theta_m \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} I_\text{in} \\ Q_\text{in} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

In the RFP each is built from four AD834 four-quadrant multipliers \R{16} and two EL2073 summing amplifiers, with \(< 5\) ns group delay, \(> 40\) MHz full-power bandwidth and \(> 50\) dB dynamic range; the four matrix weights come from 12-bit DACs \R{13}. Unlike an RF phase shifter, such a modulator rotates the phase continuously, without steps. The PEP-II RFP uses seven of them: four in the combining network and one each for the direct-loop gain and phase, the comb loop and the ripple loop \R{13}.

\subsection{D1/D2: Beam Loading --- The Dominant Disturbance}\label{sec:d1d2}


\textbf{D1 --- Steady-state beam loading}: The beam draws twice the wall-loss power. Detuning the cavity (\cref{sec:beam-loading}) cancels the reactive part of this loading at the operating current; any change of current re-creates it (\cref{sec:transient}). 

\textbf{D2 --- Transient beam loading}: Injection, top-off, beam loss, and nonuniform fills produce changes in beam loading faster than the tuner can follow. The disturbance spectrum can extend to \(f_{\mathrm{rev}}\) and higher revolution harmonics, but the resulting cavity-field perturbation is shaped by the effective cavity impedance, \(Z_{\mathrm{eff}}=Z_{\mathrm{cav}}/(1+L)\), with components far from the cavity resonance generally attenuated.

\textbf{How feedback acts on D1/D2}: The direct loop divides the cavity impedance seen by the beam by the return difference \(1 + G_\text{OL}\) (\cref{eq:zeff}). The PEP-II design applied \(\approx 15\) dB \R{13}; the SPEAR3 legacy loop runs at \(G \approx 1\) (0 dB, \cref{sec:robinson-legacy}), for which \(\lvert 1 + G_\text{OL}\rvert\) is only \(\approx 1.6\) at the carrier and \(\approx 2\) at the cavity resonance. A 1\% (5 mA) current step changes the beam-induced voltage by \(\Delta I_{b1} R_L \cos\psi_L \approx \qty{5.5}{\kilo\volt}\) per cavity, 0.8\% of \(V_\text{gap}\); the loop divides this residual by \(\lvert 1 + G_\text{OL}(0)\rvert\), and an integral path removes it in steady state. The same impedance, sampled at the synchrotron sidebands, sets the damping of the dipole (D3) and coupled-bunch (D4) modes.

\subsection{D3: Robinson Mode}\label{sec:d3}


Every bunch oscillates about the synchronous phase at \(f_s\), and the in-phase (dipole, \(n = 0\)) motion of all bunches drives the cavities at the two sidebands \(f_\text{RF} \pm f_s\). Above transition the motion is damped when

\begin{equation}\label{eq:robinson-crit}
\text{Re}\{Z_\text{eff}(\omega_\text{RF} + \omega_s)\} < \text{Re}\{Z_\text{eff}(\omega_\text{RF} - \omega_s)\}
\end{equation}
and the rate at which the cavities add to radiation damping is, to first order \R{10}, \R{14},

\begin{equation}\label{eq:robinson-rate}
\frac{1}{\tau_\text{Rob}} = -\frac{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}\,I_b}{2\,\omega_s\,(E_0/e)\,T_\text{rev}} \left[\text{Re}\{Z_\text{eff}(\omega_\text{RF} + \omega_s)\} - \text{Re}\{Z_\text{eff}(\omega_\text{RF} - \omega_s)\}\right]
\end{equation}

(positive means damping), with \(Z_\text{eff}\) the total impedance of the four cavities. A cavity tuned \emph{below} \(f_\text{RF}\) --- exactly what beam-loading compensation requires \eqref{eq:df-opt} --- has more resistance at the lower sideband, so at SPEAR3 the Robinson mode is \emph{damped}, and strongly so without feedback: \(5.8\times10^{3}\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 500 mA, 14 times radiation damping. The hazard is therefore not an instability of the beam--cavity system itself but a \emph{loss} of this damping caused by the direct loop. Feedback lowers and widens the resonance, so the damping falls roughly as \((1+G)^{-3}\), and a wrongly phased loop or integral path can reverse its sign \R{9}, \R{11}. At 500 mA the coherent frequency without feedback is pulled from \qty{10.0}{\kilo\hertz} down to \qty{6.4}{\kilo\hertz}, too large a shift for the first-order \cref{eq:robinson-rate}; \cref{sec:robinson} solves the full problem and turns it into tuning guidance. The mode is also a resonator that every disturbance near \(f_s\) drives --- the HVPS lines (D5), beam transients (D2) and the noise of the RF system (D10) --- and the motion they cause grows as its damping falls (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}).

\subsection{D4: Coupled-Bunch Modes}\label{sec:d4}


With \(h = 372\) RF buckets the beam supports 372 coupled-bunch modes \(n = 0, \ldots, h-1\) (mode \(n = -1\) is mode \(h-1\)). Mode \(n\) samples the impedance at the synchrotron sidebands of the revolution harmonics \((ph+n)\,f_\text{rev}\), and grows at the rate \R{10}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cb-mode}
\frac{1}{\tau_n} = \frac{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}\,I_b}{2\,\omega_s\,(E_0/e)\,T_\text{rev}}\sum_p\left[\text{Re}\{Z((ph+n)\omega_\text{rev}+\omega_s)\} - \text{Re}\{Z((ph+n)\omega_\text{rev}-\omega_s)\}\right]
\end{equation}

(positive means growth); \(n = 0\) is the Robinson mode of \cref{eq:robinson-rate}. At SPEAR3 the revolution frequency, 1.28 MHz, is 36 cavity half-bandwidths: only the RF harmonic itself falls inside the fundamental resonance, and every mode \(n \neq 0\) samples it at least one revolution harmonic away. The model of \cref{sec:robinson} gives a growth rate of at most \(\approx 5\;\text{s}^{-1}\) (mode \(n = -1\), from no feedback to 15 dB of loop gain), against \(424\;\text{s}^{-1}\) of radiation damping. At PEP-II, with \(f_\text{rev} = \qty{136}{\kilo\hertz}\), more than 1 A of beam and a detuning that moved the cavity resonance past the first revolution harmonic, the same rates were faster than one revolution without feedback \R{13}; that is what the PEP-II comb and woofer loops were built for, and why SPEAR3 needs neither (\cref{sec:loop-pepii}).

\subsection{D5: HVPS Ripple}\label{sec:d5}


The 12-pulse SCR rectifier produces two families of harmonics:

\textbf{12-pulse fundamental harmonics} (ideal 12-pulse cancellation):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:ripple-12p}
f_\text{12p} = 12k \times f_\text{line} = 720,\;1440,\;2160,\;\ldots\;\text{Hz}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Residual 6-pulse harmonics} (from imperfect phase balance between the two 6-pulse bridges):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:ripple-6p}
f_\text{6p} = 6k \times f_\text{line} = 360,\;720,\;1080,\;\ldots\;\text{Hz}
\end{equation}

The 360 Hz component (the first 6-pulse harmonic, absent in ideal 12-pulse operation) is the \textbf{dominant residual} and the primary target of the ripple loop. Additional low-order harmonics at 60, 120, 180 and 240 Hz may appear at reduced levels (\(\sim 20\)--\(40\) dB below 360 Hz) depending on SCR firing symmetry \R{17}, \R{18}. The ripple reaches the RF field mainly as phase modulation of the klystron output \eqref{eq:kly-ampm}. The harmonics continue far above the 2.88 kHz reach of the ripple loop (\cref{sec:loop-ripple}): on 8 October 2026 the cavity-probe spectrum showed them up to 10 kHz, and those at 8640 and 9000 Hz, which flank the coherent synchrotron frequency at 500 mA, at \(-57\) and \(-55\) dBc, 16 dB above the noise beside them. They drive most of the beam's dipole motion (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).

\subsection{D6: Klystron Gain Variation}\label{sec:d6}


As the beam current changes from 0 to 500 mA, the HVPS loop raises the cathode voltage \(V_k\) to keep the klystron drive power, and with it the margin below saturation, constant (\cref{sec:loop-hvps}). As \(V_k\) changes, the small-signal gain of the klystron (the slope of \(P_\text{out}\) against \(P_\text{in}\)) varies by up to \(\sim 7\) dB, and with it the gain of the direct loop. Gain tracking (\cref{sec:loop-gain-tracking}) compensates for it.

\subsection{D7/D8: Microphonics and Thermal Detuning}\label{sec:d7d8}


For normal-conducting copper cavities: microphonic excursions \(< 10\) Hz (negligible vs. \(\Delta f_{1/2} = 35.5\) kHz).

\textbf{Thermal detuning estimation}: The resonant frequency of a copper cavity scales inversely with its linear dimensions: \(\Delta f/f = -\alpha_\text{Cu} \cdot \Delta T\), where \(\alpha_\text{Cu} \approx 16.5 \times 10^{-6}\;/\text{°C}\) is the linear thermal expansion coefficient of copper. For a simple cavity at 476.3 MHz, this gives:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:cav-thermal}
\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial T} \approx -f_0 \cdot \alpha_\text{Cu} = -476.3\;\text{MHz} \times 16.5 \times 10^{-6} \approx -7.9\;\text{kHz/°C}
\end{equation}


\subsection{D9: Ring Circumference Change --- RF Frequency Migration}\label{sec:d9}


The RF frequency must remain an integer multiple of the revolution frequency:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:harmonic}
f_\text{RF} = h \cdot f_\text{rev} = \frac{h \cdot c}{C}
\end{equation}

The circumference \(C\) follows the tunnel and its floor, and a feedback system updates the RF master-oscillator frequency to track it. Two motions add. Within a day the ground moves with the tides, and \(f_\text{RF}\) varies --- back and forth, not as a drift --- by up to about 100 Hz in the operating history. Over a year the tunnel temperature changes, and \(f_\text{RF}\) migrates by about 4 kHz. With \(\Delta C/C = -\Delta f_\text{RF}/f_\text{RF}\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:dC-over-C}
\frac{\Delta f_\text{RF}}{f_\text{RF}} = \frac{\qty{100}{\hertz}}{476.3\;\text{MHz}} = 2.1\times10^{-7}\ \text{(daily, tidal)},
\qquad
\frac{\qty{4}{\kilo\hertz}}{476.3\;\text{MHz}} = 8.4\times10^{-6}\ \text{(yearly, thermal)},
\end{equation}
that is up to about \qty{50}{\micro\metre} of circumference within a day and about 2.0 mm over a year, the latter consistent with a seasonal tunnel temperature change of \(\sim 0.8\)°C (\(\alpha_\text{struct} \approx 10\)--\(12 \times 10^{-6}\)/°C). A longer ring needs a lower \(f_\text{RF}\).

\subsection{D10: RF Noise}\label{sec:d10}

Every element of the chain adds broadband noise to the field: the klystron and its HVPS (between and above the ripple-loop lines), the drive amplifier, the cavity probes and demodulators that form the vector sum, and the reference. The direct loop divides noise that enters ahead of the cavities by the return difference and copies noise in its measurement and reference onto the cavities through \(T\) (\cref{eq:rejection}). For the field, D10 is the floor of the regulation. For the beam it is the drive of the dipole mode, which follows it below the coherent synchrotron frequency and amplifies it near that frequency by up to \(Q\,(f_s/f_\text{coh})^2\) (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}). No loop removes D10: the gain and phase of the direct loop decide how much of it reaches the beam, and with the cavities detuned they do so differently for amplitude and phase noise. On 8 October 2026 the cavity-probe spectrum showed \(-106\) and \(-103\) dBc/Hz beside the two sidebands of the synchrotron line; this broadband noise moved the beam's phase by \(0.14\)--\(0.19^\circ\) rms at \(f_\text{coh}\), less than the HVPS harmonics beside the line (D5) did (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).

\subsection{Feedback Fundamentals: Loop Gain, Closed Loop and Effective Impedance}\label{sec:arch-fundamentals}


The three relations below apply to the RF field loops; \cref{sec:loops} gives the block-by-block form and the parameters of each loop.

\textbf{Open-loop transfer function:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gol}
G_\text{OL}(s) = C(s)\,G_\text{plant}(s),
\qquad
C(s) = G\,e^{j\theta}\,G_\text{lead}(s)\,G_\text{int}(s),
\end{equation}
with the plant of \cref{eq:plant}. The controller \(C(s)\) has four parts: the loop gain \(G\), the loop phase error \(\theta\), the lead network \(G_\text{lead}(s)\) and the integral path \(G_\text{int}(s)\). Their explicit forms, the stability margins and what is known of their SPEAR3 values are in \cref{sec:loop-direct}.

\textbf{Closed-loop transfer function:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-loop}
T(s) = \frac{G_\text{OL}(s)}{1 + G_\text{OL}(s)}
\end{equation}

At frequencies well below \(f_c\), \(|G_\text{OL}| \gg 1\) and \(T \approx 1\); above \(f_c\), \(T \to 0\).

\textbf{Disturbance rejection and noise transfer:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rejection}
\vec{V}_\text{err} = \frac{\vec{V}_\text{d}}{1 + G_\text{OL}} - T\,\bigl(\delta\vec{V}_\text{m} - \delta\vec{V}_\text{ref}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(\vec{V}_\text{d}\) is the field error that a disturbance entering ahead of the cavities --- beam loading, klystron, HVPS --- would cause with the loop open, \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{m}\) the noise in the measured vector sum and \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{ref}\) that of the reference. The loop suppresses the first by the return difference and copies the second onto the cavities: more gain cannot remove measurement or reference noise (D10). With the cavities detuned, \(G_\text{OL}\) and \(T\) are not symmetric about the carrier (\cref{fig:closed-loop-tf}). Acting differently on the upper and the lower sideband of an error, they turn part of an amplitude error into a phase error and back. For the size of the field error that does not matter; for the beam, which senses one combination of the two (\cref{sec:robinson-picture}), it does, and \cref{sec:robinson-noise} treats the two quadratures separately.

\textbf{Effective cavity impedance seen by the beam:}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeff}
Z_\text{eff}(\omega) = \frac{Z_\text{cav}(\omega)}{1 + G_\text{OL}(\omega)}
\end{equation}

This is the central result. The direct loop \hl{ \textbf{lowers and widens the cavity resonance} }: for a proportional loop of gain \(G\) at the correct phase and negligible delay, \(R_L \to R_L/(1+G)\) and \(\omega_{1/2} \to (1+G)\,\omega_{1/2}\) \R{13}, \R{12}, \R{9}. The reduction is set by the gain:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  At the cavity resonance: \(20\log_{10}(1+G)\) --- about 16 dB for the 15 dB PEP-II design, 6 dB for the present SPEAR3 legacy setting \(G \approx 1\)
\item
  Near the carrier: an integral path adds gain without limit as \(f \to 0\)
\item
  Above the crossover \(f_c\): no reduction
\end{itemize}

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Less impedance is not automatically more stability.} The Robinson damping of \cref{sec:d3} comes from the \emph{difference} of the resistive impedance at the two synchrotron sidebands. The loop that reduces the impedance also reduces that difference, and a loop phase error rotates reactive impedance into resistive impedance of either sign. \Cref{sec:robinson} quantifies both effects.
\end{keynote}

\subsection{Multi-Loop Stability: The Bandwidth Separation Principle}\label{sec:arch-multiloop}


Several loops act on one RF station, and two loops interact only where both have gain. If their crossover frequencies differ by at least a factor of ten,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:bw-sep}
\frac{f_{c,\text{fast}}}{f_{c,\text{slow}}} \geq 10 \quad \text{for adjacent loops acting on the same quantity,}
\end{equation}
the faster loop sees the slower one as frozen, the slower loop sees the faster one as a closed loop of unit gain, and each can be designed and checked on its own \R{33}. At SPEAR3 the EPICS loops are about a million times slower than the direct loop. The ripple resonators lie inside the direct loop's band but are compatible with it for another reason: each is only \(\approx 1\) Hz wide (\cref{sec:loop-ripple}), so it adds gain only on its own line. The EPICS loops are not separated in frequency among themselves, but each drives a different actuator, and the direct loop, which holds the field, decouples them.

\subsection{Direct Loop --- Wideband RF Field Regulation}\label{sec:loop-direct}


\textbf{Purpose}: Regulates the vector sum of the four cavity fields against beam loading, klystron and HVPS disturbances (D1, D2, D5, D6) and divides the cavity impedance seen by the beam by the return difference \(1 + G_\text{OL}\). At SPEAR3 it is \emph{not} needed to cure an instability: the Robinson mode is damped by the detuned cavity and the fundamental-mode coupled-bunch modes are negligible (\cref{sec:d3,sec:d4}). Its gain and phase instead decide how much of the Robinson damping survives and how strongly the noise of the RF system (D10) moves the beam, the subject of \cref{sec:robinson}.

\textbf{Signal flow}: \cref{sec:iq-chain} and \cref{fig:llrf-chain}. Because the gain and phase are applied once, before the compensation networks, the integral path is rotated together with the proportional path.

\hl{\textbf{Open-loop transfer function}} (\cref{eq:gol} written out, normalized so that \(G\) is the loop gain at the resonance of an on-tune cavity):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gol-explicit}
G_\text{OL}(s) = G\,e^{j\theta} \cdot \frac{1 + s/\omega_z}{1 + s/\omega_p} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\omega_i}{s}\right) \cdot \frac{\omega_a}{s + \omega_a} \cdot \frac{\omega_{1/2}}{s + \omega_{1/2} - j\Delta\omega} \cdot e^{-s\tau_d}
\end{equation}
where the terms are (left to right): loop gain and phase error, lead compensator, PI integrator, drive amplifier and klystron bandwidth, cavity response \eqref{eq:cav-tf}, and transport delay. At SPEAR3 both networks are on (\cref{tab:legacy-live}). The RFgui loop models \R{41} give the RFP lead network as \(\omega_z = 2\pi \times 944\) kHz and \(\omega_p = 2\pi \times 2.39\) MHz, and the integral network as a lag with a zero at 3.19 kHz and a pole at 0.26 kHz, 21.8 dB more gain at DC than at high frequency: in the form of \cref{eq:rob-loop}, \(\omega_i = 2\pi \times 2.9\) kHz with a leak \(\omega_\ell = 2\pi \times 0.26\) kHz. The measured closed-loop responses and the loop on/off measurements of June 2020 agree with this lag network (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}); the measured Robinson damping, however, matches the model only with an effective \(\omega_i \approx 2\pi \times 1\) kHz (\cref{sec:robinson-int,sec:robinson-legacy}). \Cref{tab:robinson-model} lists the values that the analysis of \cref{sec:robinson} uses.

\textbf{Stability margins}: The design targets are a phase margin of at least \(45^\circ\) and a gain margin of at least 6 dB. With the cavity contributing \(-90^\circ\) above its half-bandwidth and the delay \(-2\pi f\tau_d\), a proportional loop meets the phase-margin target up to \(\approx 15\) dB at the legacy delay of 625 ns (\cref{eq:fc-max}, \cref{tab:robinson-delay}). PEP-II applied 15 dB with less than 500 ns of delay and used its lead-lag network for extra phase margin with the cavities detuned \R{13}. At the present SPEAR3 setting \(G \approx 1\) the proportional path alone has no unity-gain crossover (\(\lvert G_\text{OL}\rvert \le 1\)); the lag network adds one at \(\approx \pm 6\) kHz on tune and \(\approx \pm 3\) kHz at 500 mA, with at least \(89^\circ\) of phase margin. The binding margin is then the \(\approx 20\) dB of gain left before the delay-limited instability. The legacy configuration routine (RFguiInvasive Configure Direct) sets the gain for a \(66^\circ\) phase margin \R{41}.

\textbf{Closed-loop response}: \Cref{fig:closed-loop-tf} shows \(T(s)\) of \cref{eq:closed-loop} for the model of \cref{tab:robinson-model}, with the RFP lead network added, under two conditions, together with the response measured on 2 October 2026 with the RFgui closed-loop routine \R{38}. With the cavities on tune and no beam the response is symmetric, and the proportional path alone would peak at \(G/(1+G)\) on resonance. A delay-free proportional loop would widen the cavity's half-bandwidth by \(1+G\), to \(\pm 67\) kHz at \(G = 0.875\); the 625 ns inside the loop, partly offset by the lead network, widen it further, to half-power points at \(\pm 77\) kHz. The response measured at 22 mA follows the model with the integral path within 1 dB and \(11^\circ\) out to \(\pm 100\) kHz but falls off faster beyond. Fitted with the full loop model, lag network included, and with gain, phase, delay, \(Q_L\) and detuning free, the six runs at 20--22 mA give \(G = 0.88 \pm 0.03\), \(624 \pm 4\) ns and \(Q_L = 6800 \pm 250\), close to the 6700 of \cref{tab:robinson-model} (\cref{tab:mdc-fits}). The routine's own resonator-times-delay fit gives \(Q = 3447\), with half-power points at \(\pm 69\) kHz, and 621 ns.

At 500 mA the cavities sit 36 kHz below \(f_\text{RF}\) and the response is asymmetric: \(\lvert T\rvert\) peaks at \(-44\) kHz, and at the carrier it is 0.45 with a phase of \(-27^\circ\). The response measured at 497 mA has a similar phase but lies 3--7 dB below the model. The difference needs no change of gain, delay or \(Q_L\): with them held at their low-current values, a klystron whose small-signal response to the drive amplitude differs from that to the drive phase reproduces the measurement (dotted; \cref{sec:robinson-legacy}). The integral path changes mainly the region within about \(\pm 10\) kHz of the carrier. The lag network raises \(\lvert T\rvert\) at the carrier to 0.92 and the rejection \(\lvert 1 + G_\text{OL}\rvert\) at 60 Hz to 22 dB on tune and 19 dB at 500 mA. At the synchrotron sidebands, \(\pm 8.8\) kHz (panels c and d), it changes \(\lvert T\rvert\) by at most 0.06 and turns the phase by about \(11^\circ\), in opposite directions at the two sidebands. In the model this is enough to make the Robinson mode unstable at 500 mA (\cref{sec:robinson-int}), because the damping is a small difference between the two sidebands; the beam is in fact well damped, and \cref{sec:robinson-legacy} discusses the discrepancy. The routine leaves out \(\pm 18\) kHz around the carrier, but the recorded signals cover it: fitted within \(\pm 25\) kHz with the proportional part held, the 22 mA records give \(f_i = 3.0\)--4.2 kHz and a leak below 0.15 kHz.

\subsection{Ripple Loop --- HVPS Harmonic Rejection}\label{sec:loop-ripple}


\textbf{Purpose}: Cancels the RF phase modulation caused by HVPS switching ripple (D5) and holds the slow phase across the klystron and drive amplifier as the cathode voltage moves (D6). The ripple appears as harmonics of 60 Hz on the klystron cathode voltage and reaches the RF through the voltage sensitivity of the klystron phase \eqref{eq:kly-ampm}.

\textbf{Signal flow}: klystron output and station reference I/Q (digitized on the RFP itself since the Rev 4 module of 2006) → DSP phase error \(e_\phi[m] = \phi_\text{ref} - \phi_\text{kly}\) → a DC term plus a bank of resonant filters at line harmonics → phase correction applied through a baseband modulator in the drive path \R{13}, \R{30}.

\textbf{Key design choice}: The loop measures the \textbf{klystron output} rather than the cavity probes, because the ripple frequencies (\(< 3\) kHz) lie well inside the cavity bandwidth and the correction must be applied upstream.

\textbf{Algorithm} (SPEAR3 firmware \texttt{sp3ripple.s} and coefficient table \texttt{Sp3PhsCoefs.tbl}, W.~Ross, 2006 \R{30}): each harmonic \(k\) is a second-order resonator acting on the phase error,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:ripple-res}
H_k(z) = \frac{c_{0k}\,z^{-1} + c_{1k}\,z^{-2}}{1 - 2r_k\cos(2\pi f_k T_s)\,z^{-1} + r_k^2\,z^{-2}},
\qquad
u_\phi = \Bigl(H_\text{DC} + \sum_k H_k\Bigr)\,e_\phi,
\end{equation}
where \(e_\phi = \phi_\text{ref} - \phi_\text{kly}\) is the phase error of the klystron output against the station reference, \(u_\phi\) is the phase correction that the DSP applies through its baseband modulator in the drive path, \(H_\text{DC}\) is the DC term that holds the slow phase, and \(T_s \approx 1/\qty{24.6}{\kilo\hertz}\) is the sampling period (one sample per 52 turns). The pole radius \(r_k^2 = 0.999838\) makes every line about 1 Hz wide, so the loop has very high gain exactly at the line harmonics and essentially none in between: it is an internal-model (repetitive) controller, not a broadband loop. The numerator coefficients \(c_{0k}, c_{1k}\) set the gain and phase of each line to suit the drive-path phase at that frequency. The 2006 revision removed the amplitude estimator to make room for double-precision phase arithmetic, so \textbf{the loop corrects phase ripple only}.

\textbf{Harmonics}: four ``slow'' lines at 60, 120, 180 and 240 Hz, updated in turn at a quarter of the sample rate, and 26 ``fast'' lines between 300 Hz and 2.88 kHz (every 60 Hz up to 960 Hz, sparser above). The table also lists eight lines between 5.76 and 7.2 kHz that the SPEAR3 build does not process.

\subsection{Gain Tracking}\label{sec:loop-gain-tracking}


\textbf{Purpose}: Keeps the gain of the direct loop's forward path constant while the klystron gain changes with cathode voltage by up to \(\sim 7\) dB (D6).

\textbf{Implementation}: Every 2 s (\texttt{STNRIPPLE:\allowbreak{}LOOP:\allowbreak{}READY}) the EPICS subroutine \texttt{subSysDCcoeff} \R{37} reads the klystron gain \(K_\text{kly}\) in dB, from the forward and drive power monitors, and moves the DC coefficient of the ripple DSP towards

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain-track}
c_\text{DC} = c_\text{max}\,10^{(K_\text{kly,set} - K_\text{kly})/20},
\end{equation}
so that \(c_\text{DC}\) times the klystron gain stays constant. Each step is limited by a deadband in dB and by a maximum change of \(c_\text{DC}\), and tracking pauses during a beam abort or when the klystron gain is low (park). The DSP applies this coefficient, together with its harmonic phase corrections, through its baseband modulator in the drive path \R{13}. The RFguiInvasive Configure Direct routine (\texttt{ConfDirect} in \R{12}) sets the loop gain, the loop phase and the starting point of the gain tracking \R{41}.

\subsection{HVPS Loop --- Klystron Operating Point}\label{sec:loop-hvps}


\textbf{Purpose}: Adjusts the cathode voltage \(V_k\) to keep the klystron drive power constant, so that the klystron stays \(\sim 10\%\) below saturation as the beam current changes (D6) and the direct loop keeps headroom for fast corrections.

\textbf{Signal flow}: klystron drive power (or the station gap voltage) → EPICS SNL \texttt{rf\_\allowbreak{}hvps\_\allowbreak{}loop.\allowbreak{}st,\allowbreak{}v} → PLC voltage setpoint → Enerpro SCR firing angle → \(V_k\) \R{12}.

\end{equation}
clamped to a maximum step per cycle. A cycle runs each time the station-voltage readback updates --- ``usually every 0.5 second'' in the program's own description --- and at least every 10 s.

\subsection{Tuner Loop --- Cavity Resonant Frequency Tracking}\label{sec:loop-tuner}


\textbf{Purpose}: Moves each cavity tuner to hold the optimum detuning angle \(\psi_\text{opt}\) \eqref{eq:psi-opt-eval} as the beam current changes (the dominant term), and follows cavity thermal drift (D8) and the migration of \(f_\text{RF}\) (D9).

\textbf{Signal flow}: cavity probe phase and forward phase → difference (the load angle) → comparison with \(\psi_\text{target}\) → deadband → stepper motor → tuner plunger (\cref{sec:tuner-mech}).

\textbf{Control law} (\texttt{rf\_\allowbreak{}tuner\_\allowbreak{}loop.\allowbreak{}st,\allowbreak{}v} \R{28}, executed every 2 s):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tuner-err}
\varepsilon = \left[\angle(V_\text{probe}) - \angle(V_\text{fwd})\right] - \psi_\text{target}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tuner-step}
\text{Step command} = \begin{cases} +N_\text{step} & \text{if } \varepsilon > \varepsilon_\text{deadband} \\ -N_\text{step} & \text{if } \varepsilon < -\varepsilon_\text{deadband} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Frequency offset estimate} (\texttt{subSysFreqOff} \R{37}):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:tuner-poly}
\Delta f_\text{est}(x, V) = p_0 + p_1(x - x_\text{home}) + p_2(x - x_\text{home})^2 + p_3(x - x_\text{home})^3 + t_1 V^2
\end{equation}
where \(x\) and \(x_\text{home}\) are the tuner position and its home position in mm, \(p_0\ldots p_3\) are calibration coefficients (\(p_1 \approx 20\)--30 kHz/mm) and \(t_1 V^2\) is a term in the cavity voltage (named a temperature coefficient in the code). Both are written by the RFguiInvasive Make Polynomials routine, which measures each cavity's resonance over the tuner range with the direct loop open, fits the cubic, and derives \(t_1\) from the shift once the cavities are at voltage \R{41}. The estimate is smoothed exponentially. The tuner loop itself servos on the load angle; the estimate is used to park the cavities and by the direct loop's frequency-offset tracking (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).

\subsection{PEP-II Loops Not Installed at SPEAR3}\label{sec:loop-pepii}

The PEP-II RFP serves three more loops \R{13}. Their VXI modules --- two comb modules and the gap-voltage feed-forward (GVF) module --- are absent from the SPEAR3 crate \R{36}, and SPEAR3 does not need them.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Comb loop.} Second-order IIR filters, one each for I and Q, with peaks at the synchrotron sidebands of the low-order revolution harmonics and a zero at the harmonics themselves, followed by a 32-tap FIR group-delay equalizer (phase linear to \(< 10^\circ\) over 4 MHz) and a one-turn delay with a 25 ns vernier \R{13}. Because it has gain only near each sideband it is not limited by the loop delay, and at PEP-II it added \(\approx 20\) dB of impedance reduction to the \(\approx 16\) dB of the direct loop. At SPEAR3 the fundamental-mode coupled-bunch growth is \(\lesssim 5\;\text{s}^{-1}\) (\cref{sec:d4}).
\item \textbf{Longitudinal ``woofer''.} The bunch-by-bunch longitudinal feedback sent a band-limited (4 MHz) kick signal over a 10-bit, 10 MHz fiber link; equalized and delayed like the comb, it modulated the station reference and damped the low-order modes (\(\lvert n\rvert < 10\)) with 30 dB of loop gain \R{13}, \R{34}. SPEAR3 has no longitudinal feedback system.
\item \textbf{Gap feed-forward.} A DSP in the GVF module generated a station reference that varied within one turn to follow the transient of the PEP-II ion-clearing gap, adapting over 100 ms \R{13}. SPEAR3 has no ion-clearing gap; its reference comes from a pair of RFP DACs set by the DAC loop \R{36}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Transient Operation: Matching Under Changing Beam Current and RF Frequency}\label{sec:transient}


Everything in \cref{sec:beam-loading} is a \emph{steady-state} result: it describes the station at one beam current, with the cavity already sitting at the detuning that matches it. Operationally the station is almost never in that state. Three things move the operating point, on three very different timescales:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Beam current} changes over a fill, a top-off shot or a beam loss (D1, D2). It fixes the fundamental beam current \(I_{b1} = 2I_b\) and hence the detuning the cavity needs.
\item
  \textbf{The RF frequency} follows the ring circumference (D9, \cref{sec:d9}): up to about 100 Hz within a day as the ground moves with the tides, and about 4 kHz over a year as the tunnel temperature changes. The cavity has to follow \(f_\text{RF}\), not the other way round.
\item
  \textbf{The cavity resonance} itself drifts with copper temperature and moves with microphonics (D7, D8).
\end{itemize}

All three arrive at the coupler as the same single quantity --- a detuning error --- and all three are corrected by the same single actuator, the mechanical tuner. What distinguishes them is only how fast they move and how far.

\subsection{Reflected Power as a Function of Detuning and Beam Current}\label{sec:transient-gamma}


With the direct loop holding the gap voltage at \(V_\text{gap}\), the load presented to the input coupler is the cavity in parallel with the beam. Normalizing the total gap admittance to the external (coupler) conductance \(\beta G_c\), and introducing the two dimensionless beam-loading parameters together with the detuning variable

\begin{equation}\label{eq:blparams}
a \equiv \frac{I_{b1} R_s \cos\phi_s}{V_\text{gap}} = \frac{P_\text{beam}}{P_\text{wall}},
\qquad
b \equiv \frac{I_{b1} R_s \sin\phi_s}{V_\text{gap}},
\qquad
t \equiv \tan\psi_0 = \frac{2 Q_0 \Delta f}{f_0},
\end{equation}
the normalized load admittance is \(y_L = \left[(1+a) + j(t+b)\right]/\beta\), and the fraction of forward power reflected at the coupler is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
|\Gamma|^2 = \left\lvert\frac{1-y_L}{1+y_L}\right\rvert^2
 = \frac{(\beta - 1 - a)^2 + (t+b)^2}{(\beta + 1 + a)^2 + (t+b)^2}
\end{equation}

At 500 mA, \(a = 2.00\) and \(b = 4.84\). Two consequences follow immediately.

\textbf{The tuner owns the imaginary part.} \Cref{eq:gamma} is minimized over tuning when \(t = -b\), which is precisely the optimum detuning of \cref{eq:psi-opt}. Driving \(t+b\) to zero is the entire job of the tuner loop, and it is the \emph{only} loop that can do it.

\textbf{The coupler sets the floor.} At \(t = -b\) the residual reflection is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-min}
|\Gamma|^2_\text{min} = \left(\frac{\beta - 1 - a}{\beta + 1 + a}\right)^2
\end{equation}
which vanishes only when \(\beta = 1 + a\). Perfect matching at 500 mA would need \(\beta_\text{opt} = 3.00\); the installed PEP-II coupler gives \(\beta = 3.78\). The station is therefore \textbf{over-coupled at its design current} and carries a residual \(|\Gamma|^2_\text{min} = \qty{1.3}{\percent}\).

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Forward power with the coupler floor.} With the field held at \(V_\text{gap}\), the cavity and beam absorb \(P_\text{wall} + P_\text{beam}/n_\text{cav} = \qty{204.3}{\kilo\watt}\) per cavity \eqref{eq:pgen-eval}, so the klystron must deliver
\(P_\text{fwd} = 204.3/(1-|\Gamma|^2_\text{min}) = 204.3/0.987 = \qty{207}{\kilo\watt}\) per cavity. On 7 October 2026 the four cavities took 193--214 kW at 499 mA, \qty{207}{\kilo\watt} on average, with 0.9--3.1\% reflected; the coupling factors inferred from those reflections, \(\beta = 3.6\)--4.3 with a median of 3.74, agree with the PEP-II value (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).
\end{keynote}

\subsection{Required Detuning Versus Beam Current}\label{sec:transient-detuning}


Because \(\phi_s\) depends only on \(U_0\) and \(V_\text{RF}\), and not on current, the detuning the cavity needs is \textbf{linear in beam current}: \(\Delta f_\text{opt} = -\qty{72.0}{\hertz\per\milli\ampere} \times I_b\). \Cref{fig:detuning-vs-current} shows both the frequency offset and the loaded angle the tuner servos on. Filling from zero to 500 mA therefore requires the tuner to walk the cavity through the full \qty{36.0}{\kilo\hertz}, about one cavity half-bandwidth; the loaded angle swings from \(0^\circ\) to \(-45.4^\circ\).

\subsection{Division of Labor: the Direct Loop Holds the Field, the Tuner Restores the Match}\label{sec:transient-roles}


Two loops respond to these transients, and it is worth stating plainly that neither can do the other's job.

\textbf{The direct loop regulates the field, and it is fast.} With a closed-loop half-bandwidth of about \qty{70}{\kilo\hertz} at the present legacy gain (up to \(\approx 0.2\) MHz at the delay limit, \cref{eq:fc-max}) it corrects a beam-current step within a few microseconds --- faster than the \qty{4.5}{\micro\second} cavity fill time \eqref{eq:cav-bw}, and far faster than any mechanical motion. But it holds \(V_\text{gap}\) by \emph{demanding more generator current}. It does not change \(y_L\): the cavity is exactly as mismatched as before, and the extra forward power is delivered, reflected, and dumped in the circulator load. \textbf{A field-regulation loop cannot reduce reflected power.}

\textbf{The tuner restores the match, and it is slow.} Only the tuner changes \(t\), and it does so through a stepper motor and a mechanical plunger, with a 2 s cycle and a bandwidth of 0.01--0.05 Hz (\cref{sec:loop-tuner,sec:tuner-mech}) --- six to seven decades slower than the direct loop.

The resulting sequence for any current transient is therefore always the same:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  The beam current changes, which changes \(a\) and \(b\) essentially instantaneously.
\item
  The direct loop holds \(V_\text{gap}\) through the change. Field amplitude and phase never leave specification.
\item
  The reflected power jumps to the value given by \cref{eq:gamma} at the \emph{old} tuning, and the klystron carries the extra forward power.
\item
  The tuner walks \(t\) back to \(-b\) over many 2 s cycles, and the reflected power decays to the coupler floor.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{The separation of timescales is what makes this safe.} Because the direct loop is more than \(10^6\) times faster than the tuner, the two never interact dynamically --- the tuner sees a cavity whose field is already regulated, and the direct loop sees a detuning that is constant on its own timescale. That is the same bandwidth-separation argument as \cref{eq:bw-sep}, applied to the two loops that act on the same cavity. The cost of the separation is that mismatch is corrected slowly, so transient reflected power must be budgeted as a hardware rating rather than regulated away.
\end{keynote}

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Not covered here}: this analysis is quasi-static in the tuner variable --- it evaluates \cref{eq:gamma} at each tuning, but does not model the tuner's own closed-loop trajectory, its deadband limit cycle, or the coupled dynamics of four independently tuned cavities driven from one klystron. A measured tuner step response and a fill record with synchronized current, forward and reflected power would let those be added; neither is currently in the repository.
\end{keynote}

\section{Synchrotron Damping and Direct-Loop Optimization}\label{sec:robinson}

The dipole synchrotron mode of the stored beam --- all bunches oscillating in phase, coupled-bunch mode \(n = 0\) --- is coupled to the fundamental mode of the four cavities and, through them, to the direct loop. Its coherent frequency and damping rate are therefore the most direct beam-based measure of how the direct loop is set. A correctly phased loop of moderate gain leaves the mode strongly damped; a mis-phased or over-strong loop, or an integral path with the wrong phase, removes the damping that the detuned cavity would otherwise provide. The mode is also a driven oscillator: the noise of the RF system moves the beam by an amount that grows as the damping falls, and the same loop decides how much of that noise reaches it. This section states that picture (\cref{sec:robinson-picture}), gives the complete small-signal model (\cref{sec:robinson-model}), its predictions as functions of beam current, loop gain, loop phase, delay and integral path (\crefrange{sec:robinson-current}{sec:robinson-int}) and the beam's response to noise (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}), compares them with what is known about the legacy station today (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}), and turns the result into a procedure for setting the loop (\cref{sec:robinson-procedure}). Every number is computed from the model of \cref{sec:robinson-model} with the parameters of \cref{sec:ring-params,sec:cavity-circuit}, unless it is marked as measured.

\subsection{Beam Dipole Motion as a Driven, Damped Oscillator}\label{sec:robinson-picture}

Linearized about the synchronous phase, the dipole motion of the beam --- the phase \(\Delta\phi\) of the bunch centroids, positive when late --- obeys the equation of a driven, damped oscillator (Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-driven}
\frac{d^2\Delta\phi}{dt^2} + 2\sigma\,\frac{d\Delta\phi}{dt} + \bigl(\omega_\text{coh}^2 + \sigma^2\bigr)\,\Delta\phi \simeq \omega_s^2\,n_\text{eff}(t),
\qquad
n_\text{eff} = \varepsilon_A\cot\phi_s - \varepsilon_\phi .
\end{equation}
The left side is the beam together with the cavities and the loop. Its damping rate \(\sigma\) and coherent frequency \(\omega_\text{coh}\) are the root of the characteristic equation of \cref{sec:robinson-model}, which contains the cavity impedance, the loop and the beam current; exactly, \(D_0(s)\,\Delta\phi = \omega_s^2\,n_\text{eff}\) with \(D_0\) of \cref{eq:rob-char}, and \cref{eq:rob-driven} keeps its dominant root. The right side is the drive: the relative amplitude error \(\varepsilon_A\) and the phase error \(\varepsilon_\phi\) (positive = advance, \cref{eq:iq-def}) of the total gap voltage that the RF system produces with the bunches held at the synchronous phase. The voltage that the oscillation induces itself is already in \(D_0\), through \(Z_b\), and must not be counted again as drive; a gap error measured with beam contains both. At \(\phi_s = 67.5^\circ\), \(\cot\phi_s = 0.41\): a 0.1\% amplitude error moves the beam as much as a \(0.024^\circ\) phase error, and once the errors are at the gap the beam cannot tell them apart.

A bunch executing phase oscillations at \(\omega_s\) modulates the beam current at \(\omega_\text{RF} \pm \omega_s\), and the cavity answers at both sidebands. The resistive part of that answer exchanges energy with the oscillation: above transition the motion is damped when the cavity presents less resistance at the upper sideband than at the lower one \eqref{eq:robinson-crit}, which is the case for a cavity tuned below \(f_\text{RF}\). The reactive part shifts the coherent frequency, and at SPEAR3 lowers it. Because beam-loading compensation already tunes each cavity about one half-bandwidth below \(f_\text{RF}\) \eqref{eq:df-opt}, the SPEAR3 beam is strongly Robinson-damped without any feedback. The second Robinson limit --- the restoring force cancelled at zero frequency by the reactive beam loading --- would be reached only at 1.19 A with the cavities at the optimum detuning.

The direct loop acts on both sides of \cref{eq:rob-driven}. Through the impedance it presents to the beam, \(Z_\text{eff} = Z_\text{cav}/(1 + G_\text{OL})\) \eqref{eq:zeff}, it sets \(\sigma\) and \(\omega_\text{coh}\); through the way it passes the noise of the RF system to the gap \eqref{eq:rejection}, it sets \(n_\text{eff}\). Four consequences set every rule in \cref{sec:robinson-procedure}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The loop \textbf{lowers and widens} the resonance, so the impedance difference between the two sidebands --- and with it the damping --- falls steeply with gain, approaching pure radiation damping at high gain.
\item A loop \textbf{phase error}, and the loop \textbf{delay}, rotate \(Z_\text{eff}\) and convert reactive impedance into resistive impedance of one sign or the other; whether that adds or removes damping depends on the sign of the error.
\item An \textbf{integral path} adds large gain close to the carrier with a phase fixed by the loop. Around a detuned cavity that phase is not the one that damps the beam, so a strong integral path can make the Robinson mode unstable while it improves field regulation.
\item The beam's response to a drive near \(\omega_\text{coh}\) grows as \(1/\sigma\), so the loop's \textbf{noise transfer} and the damping it leaves cannot be chosen separately. With the cavities detuned the loop treats amplitude and phase noise differently, and more gain can leave the beam moving more although the field is quieter (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Small-Signal Model}\label{sec:robinson-model}

\textbf{Cavities and loop.} Each cavity \(k\) is the baseband resonator of \cref{eq:cav-tf}, \(Z_k(s) = R_L\omega_{1/2}/(s + \omega_{1/2} - j\Delta\omega_k)\), with \(s\) the Laplace variable of the RF envelope. One klystron drives all four cavities, and the loop measures their vector sum with weights \(w_k\) (\(\sum_k w_k = 1\)). Its open-loop transfer function is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-loop}
G_\text{OL}(s) = C(s)\,\frac{\omega_a}{s + \omega_a}\,e^{-s\tau_d}\,\frac{1}{R_L}\sum_k w_k Z_k(s),
\qquad
C(s) = G e^{j\theta}\left[1 + e^{j\Delta\theta_i}\,\frac{\omega_i}{s + \omega_\ell}\right] G_\text{lead}(s),
\end{equation}
where \(G\) is the loop gain at the resonance of an on-tune cavity, \(\theta\) the loop phase error there (positive = lead), \(\omega_a\) the bandwidth of drive amplifier and klystron (2.5 MHz assumed), \(\tau_d\) the total delay and \(G_\text{lead}\) the lead network. The integral path equals the proportional path in magnitude at \(f_i = \omega_i/2\pi\); \(\Delta\theta_i\) is its phase \emph{relative} to the proportional path --- zero in the legacy RFP, where one modulator rotates both paths (\cref{sec:loop-direct}), but an independent setting in the LLRF9 --- and \(\omega_\ell\) allows for a leaky integrator. \Cref{eq:rob-loop} is \cref{eq:gol-explicit} generalized to four cavities that need not share one detuning.

\textbf{Impedance seen by the beam.} With the field loop closed the beam drives the cavities through

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-zb}
Z_b(s) = \frac{\sum_k Z_k(s)}{1 + G_\text{OL}(s)},
\end{equation}
which is exact for one generator current shared by all cavities: the loop cannot tell beam-induced from generator-induced voltage, so it divides the total by the return difference.

\textbf{Beam dynamics.} For rigid bunches in a uniform fill, coupled-bunch mode \(n\) evolves as \(e^{st}\), where \(s\) is a root of

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-char}
D_n(s) \equiv s^2 + 2\alpha_\text{rad}\,s + \omega_s^2 - j\kappa\left[Z_b(s + jn\omega_\text{rev}) - \overline{Z_b(\bar s - jn\omega_\text{rev})}\right] = 0
\end{equation}
with

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-coeffs}
\kappa = \frac{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}\,I_b}{(E_0/e)\,T_\text{rev}},\qquad
\omega_s^2 = \frac{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}\,V_\text{RF}\sin\phi_s}{(E_0/e)\,T_\text{rev}},\qquad
\alpha_\text{rad} = \frac{J_s U_0}{2E_0 T_\text{rev}}.
\end{equation}
\Cref{eq:rob-char} follows from the linearized equation of synchrotron motion \R{8}, with the beam-induced voltage fed back through \(Z_b\); Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq} gives the derivation.

At 500 mA, \(\kappa = 754\;\Omega^{-1}\text{s}^{-2}\), \(\omega_s/2\pi = \qty{10.03}{\kilo\hertz}\) and \(\alpha_\text{rad} = 424\;\text{s}^{-1}\). The root connected continuously to the incoherent oscillator, \(s = -\sigma + j\omega_\text{coh}\), gives the coherent frequency and the amplitude damping rate \(\sigma\) (\(\sigma > 0\): damped). The line seen on a probe spectrum has \(Q = \omega_\text{coh}/2\sigma\), so a Lorentzian fit of \(f\) and \(Q\) gives \(\sigma = \pi f/Q\).

Three limits make \cref{eq:rob-char} familiar:

\begin{itemize}
\item Without feedback it is exactly Pedersen's quartic for a beam-loaded cavity \R{35}, and at low current it reproduces the Robinson rate of \R{10}.
\item When the coherent shift is small it reduces to the first-order sideband formula, the baseband form of \cref{eq:robinson-rate}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-first}
\sigma \simeq \alpha_\text{rad} - \frac{\kappa}{2\omega_s}\bigl[\text{Re}\,Z_b(j\omega_s) - \text{Re}\,Z_b(-j\omega_s)\bigr],
\qquad
\omega_\text{coh} \simeq \omega_s + \frac{\kappa}{2\omega_s}\bigl[\text{Im}\,Z_b(j\omega_s) + \text{Im}\,Z_b(-j\omega_s)\bigr]
\end{equation}
\item At \(s = 0\) it gives the second (static) Robinson condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-static}
D_0(0) = \omega_s^2 + 2\kappa\,\text{Im}\,Z_b(0) > 0,
\end{equation}
which any integral path satisfies automatically, since it makes \(Z_b(0) = 0\).
\end{itemize}

A time-domain simulation of the same system --- rigid bunch, four cavities, delayed loop --- agrees with the roots of \cref{eq:rob-char} to 0.2\% in frequency and 1\% in damping. \Cref{eq:rob-first} is adequate once the loop gain is 0 dB or more; without feedback at 500 mA it gets the damping right to within 3\% but puts the coherent line at 7.2 kHz instead of 6.4 kHz, so the full equation is used throughout.

\textbf{Why the damping falls with gain.} For an ideal proportional loop (no delay, \(\theta = 0\), identical cavities) the closed loop simply replaces \(R_L \to R_L/(1+G)\) and \(\omega_{1/2} \to (1+G)\,\omega_{1/2}\) at unchanged \(\Delta\omega\). \Cref{eq:rob-first} then gives, for \(\omega_s \ll (1+G)\,\omega_{1/2}\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-ideal}
\sigma - \alpha_\text{rad} \simeq -\frac{2\,\kappa\,n_\text{cav}\,R_L}{(1+G)^2\,\omega_{1/2}}\,\sin\psi'\cos^3\psi',
\qquad \tan\psi' = \frac{\tan\psi_L}{1+G},
\end{equation}
which is positive for \(\psi_L < 0\) and falls as \((1+G)^{-3}\) once \(G \gg \lvert\tan\psi_L\rvert\). At 500 mA, with \(\alpha_\text{rad}\) added back, it gives \(\sigma = 5.6\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) without feedback and \(\sigma = 2.1\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at \(G = 1\); the delay of the real loop removes a further 10--15\%.

\textbf{Parameters used.} The lead and integral networks of the legacy RFP are switched on. The measured loop responses agree with the component values of the RFgui loop models (\cref{sec:loop-direct,sec:robinson-legacy}), but with that integral network the model gives far less Robinson damping than is measured (\cref{sec:robinson-int}). The calculations therefore leave out the lead network, which changes the damping by about 2\%, and include an integral path only where it is varied deliberately; \cref{tab:robinson-model} lists every value used.

\subsection{Dependence on Loop Gain and Phase}\label{sec:robinson-gain}

\Cref{fig:robinson-gain} shows the damping at 500 mA as the gain is raised, for loop-phase errors up to \(\pm 30^\circ\); each curve stops where the field loop itself becomes unstable. \Cref{fig:robinson-phase} shows the damping at the present gain of 0 dB as a function of the phase error, from 100 to 500 mA. Five rules follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Gain buys impedance reduction and costs damping.} At the correct phase the damping falls from \(3.2\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at \(-6\) dB to \(1.9\times10^3\) at 0 dB, 866 at 6 dB and \(434\;\text{s}^{-1}\) --- radiation damping --- at 15 dB.
\item \textbf{Above about \(-4\) dB, a lead error adds damping and a lag error removes it, and the asymmetry grows with gain.} At 500 mA and 0 dB a \(30^\circ\) error changes the damping by \(-20\%\) (lag) or \(+9\%\) (lead); at 6 dB a \(30^\circ\) lag leaves \(220\;\text{s}^{-1}\), less than radiation damping, so the cavities then \emph{anti}-damp the beam.
\item \textbf{A lag error is most dangerous at low current}, where the detuning, and with it the Robinson damping, is small. At 0 dB the cavities stop adding damping (\(\sigma = \alpha_\text{rad}\)) at \(\theta \approx -9^\circ\) for 100 mA, \(-21^\circ\) for 200 mA, \(-31^\circ\) for 300 mA, \(-45^\circ\) for 400 mA and \(-65^\circ\) for 500 mA, and the beam becomes Robinson-unstable (\(\sigma < 0\)) beyond \(-40^\circ\) to \(-55^\circ\) below 500 mA (\(-75^\circ\) at 500 mA). A lead of up to \(+90^\circ\) increases the damping at every current.
\item \textbf{The delay acts as a frequency-dependent phase error} \(-\omega\tau_d\): about \(-2^\circ\) at the synchrotron sidebands, but \(+8^\circ\) at the operating detuning of \(-36\) kHz, so a loop phased on an on-tune cavity runs slightly ahead at the operating point. The legacy frequency-offset tracking is designed to remove such shifts (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}); at the present gain either sign changes the damping by less than 5\%.
\item \textbf{The field loop goes unstable first, at 17--20 dB} with this delay (\cref{sec:robinson-delay}), well above any gain worth running at SPEAR3.
\end{enumerate}

Three conclusions follow.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Shared phase (legacy architecture).} An integral path rotated together with the proportional path (\(\Delta\theta_i = 0\)) removes damping fast: \(1.1\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at \(f_i = 1\) kHz, \(280\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 2 kHz, and the beam is Robinson-unstable at 3 kHz. A scan of the common loop phase from \(-75^\circ\) to \(+90^\circ\) finds no setting that rescues a 3 kHz integral path at this current. In this model, a beam that is stable with \texttt{INTCOMP} on needs an integral network that is weak at the synchrotron frequency, equivalent to \(f_i \lesssim 2\) kHz, and the measured damping corresponds to about 1 kHz (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}). The measured loop responses, however, indicate the lag network of the RFgui loop models \R{41}, equivalent to \(f_i \approx 2.9\) kHz with a 0.26 kHz leak (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}). With it the model makes the beam Robinson-unstable (\(\sigma \approx -390\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at \(\theta = 0\), and no damping even at a \(40^\circ\) lead), whereas the measured damping is about \(10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\). The model therefore misses a damping contribution at high current, and 1 kHz is an effective value (\cref{sec:robinson-open}). Read as \(f_i = 30\) kHz, the PEP-II ``30 kHz bandwidth integrator'' \R{13} would be unstable here.
\item \textbf{Rotated phase.} Rotating the integral path ahead of the proportional path by about the load angle (\(\Delta\theta_i = +45^\circ\)) keeps a 3 kHz path damped, and \(+90^\circ\) turns even a 10 kHz path into strong additional damping. This freedom exists only where the integral path has its own phase control, as in the LLRF9.
\item \textbf{Ripple rejection needs a strong integral path or a canceller.} The proportional loop alone rejects the 360 and 720 Hz lines by only \(\approx 4\) dB, and 20--26 dB needs \(f_i \approx 10\) kHz. The legacy station combines its lag network with the narrowband DSP canceller (\cref{sec:loop-ripple}). The LLRF9 must get it from its integral path, and must therefore set that path's phase with the Robinson mode in mind.
\item \textbf{The integral path also sets two parts of the beam's jitter} (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}). Its gain at the line harmonics lowers the timing jitter the beam inherits from them, which no damping reduces; a shared phase lowers \(\sigma\) and so raises the resonant motion near \(f_\text{coh}\). \Cref{eq:rob-target} weighs the two, and with an independent phase \(\Delta\theta_i\) the LLRF9 can improve both.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Noise-Driven Motion and the Gain Trade-off}\label{sec:robinson-noise}

\Cref{eq:rob-driven} turns the damping into a noise problem. This subsection gives the beam's response to a drive, how the noise of the RF system reaches the drive through the loop, and what follows for the loop gain. Spectral densities are one-sided and per hertz: \(S_x(f)\) for a signal \(x\), so that \(\langle x^2\rangle = \int_0^\infty S_x\,df\).

\textbf{Response.} The beam's phase follows the drive through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-response}
\chi(s) = \frac{\Delta\phi(s)}{n_\text{eff}(s)} = \frac{\omega_s^2}{D_0(s)},
\qquad
S_{\Delta\phi}(f) = \lvert\chi(j2\pi f)\rvert^2\,S_n(f).
\end{equation}
Near the coherent frequency \(\lvert\chi\rvert^2\) is a Lorentzian of full width \(\sigma/\pi\) --- the line of a probe spectrum (\cref{sec:robinson-model}) --- with a peak \(\lvert\chi\rvert_\text{max} \simeq Q\,(\omega_s/\omega_\text{coh})^2\). For a drive whose spectral density is flat across the line,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-var}
\boxed{\langle\Delta\phi^2\rangle = \frac{\omega_s^4}{8\,\omega_\text{coh}^2}\,\frac{S_n(f_\text{coh})}{\sigma}}
\end{equation}
that is, \(\lvert\chi\rvert_\text{max}^2\,S_n\) over a noise bandwidth of \(\sigma/2\) (in hertz, with \(\sigma\) in \(\text{s}^{-1}\)). At 500 mA it agrees with the integral of the exact \(\lvert\chi\rvert^2\) to 3\% without feedback (\(Q = 3.5\)) and to 1\% from 0 dB of gain upwards. For the line of 8 October 2026, \(\lvert\chi\rvert_\text{max} = 33\) and the noise bandwidth is 540 Hz (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).

Below the line the beam follows the drive: \(\chi(0) = \omega_s^2/D_0(0)\) is the inverse of the static ratio of \cref{tab:robinson-500} (2.46 without feedback, 1.31 at 0 dB) and exactly 1 with an integral path. The line harmonics of D5, all below 2.9 kHz, therefore move the beam's phase as much as they move the gap's, whatever the damping; only their rejection helps. A line of rms drive \(n_L\) at \(\omega_L\) gives \(\Delta\phi_L = \lvert\chi(j\omega_L)\rvert\,n_L \simeq \omega_s^2 n_L/\sqrt{(\omega_\text{coh}^2 - \omega_L^2)^2 + 4\sigma^2\omega_L^2}\), which depends on where the coherent frequency, moving with current and gain, lies relative to the line.

\textbf{What the motion means.} Each frequency component of \(\Delta\phi\) carries an energy deviation \(\Delta E/E_0 = (\omega/\alpha_c\omega_\text{RF})\,\Delta\phi\), from \(d\Delta\phi/dt = \alpha_c\omega_\text{RF}\,\Delta E/E_0\) (Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq}), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-energy}
S_{\Delta E/E_0}(f) = \Bigl(\frac{2\pi f}{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}}\Bigr)^2 S_{\Delta\phi}(f),
\end{equation}
0.016 per radian at 8.8 kHz; where the dispersion \(\eta\) is not zero the orbit moves by \(\eta\,\Delta E/E_0\). The timing jitter is \(\Delta\phi/\omega_\text{RF}\), 0.58 ps per \(0.1^\circ\). The two weight the spectrum differently: the timing contains everything the beam follows below the line, the energy deviation essentially only the resonant part, and for a flat drive \(\langle(\Delta E/E_0)^2\rangle = \omega_s^4\,S_n(f_\text{coh})/[8\sigma(\alpha_c\omega_\text{RF})^2]\) does not depend on \(\omega_\text{coh}\). The photon-energy jitter, \(2\,\Delta E/E_0\), is of order \(10^{-4}\) and negligible; timing jitter and source motion are the effects that users see.

\textbf{Where the drive comes from.} The drive is the gap error that the RF system produces with the bunches held fixed. Each noise source \(x\) --- amplitude or phase noise at the klystron output, noise ahead of the klystron, noise in the measured vector sum, noise of the reference --- reaches it through a transfer function \(h_x\) of its own, and for independent sources
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-drive}
n_\text{eff}(s) = \sum_x h_x(s)\,u_x(s),
\qquad
S_n(f) = \sum_x \lvert h_x(j2\pi f)\rvert^2\,S_{u_x}(f).
\end{equation}
Drives that are not RF noise --- injection transients and changes of the fill --- add to it, and a measurement must avoid them. For a loop symmetric about the carrier, \(h_x\) is the open-loop path times \(1/(1 + G_\text{OL})\) for noise ahead of the cavities and \(T\) for measurement and reference noise \eqref{eq:rejection}. At SPEAR3 the loop is not symmetric. The cavities sit 36 kHz below \(f_\text{RF}\), so the detuned cavity turns klystron amplitude noise into gap phase noise and back through its load angle (\(\psi_L = -45^\circ\)); the loop acts differently on the two sidebands of every error; and near saturation the klystron passes amplitude and phase corrections with different gains (\cref{sec:robinson-open}). Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq} gives the two-quadrature form of \(D_0\) and \(h_x\) that includes all three; for a linear klystron its \(D_0\) is \cref{eq:rob-char}. \Cref{tab:robinson-drive} compares it with the scalar estimate for noise at the klystron output.

Without feedback, klystron amplitude noise drives the beam 5.5 times more strongly, in power, than the same klystron phase noise: the load angle turns it into gap phase. Feedback reduces the amplitude drive by more than the scalar estimate and the phase drive by much less --- at 0 dB not at all. \textbf{The trade-off.} \Cref{fig:robinson-noise} combines the drive with the response: the beam's phase and energy jitter at 500 mA for white noise from each source, as functions of the proportional gain. Three regimes follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Measurement and reference noise always moves the beam more as the gain rises}: \(T \to 1\) while \(\sigma\) falls. From 0 to 6 dB the timing variance it causes grows 4.9-fold for phase noise and 2.8-fold for amplitude noise, and by 15 dB 20-fold and 5.5-fold.
\item \textbf{Klystron amplitude noise is reduced at every gain}: to 0.44 of its timing variance without feedback at 0 dB and to 0.08 at 15 dB (energy: 0.82 and 0.18).
\item \textbf{Klystron phase noise moves the beam more with feedback than without}, at every gain up to the delay limit: 1.8 times the timing variance without feedback at 0 dB, 2.2 times at 6 dB and 1.15 times at 15 dB, and 3.0, 4.2 and 2.4 times the energy variance. The loop removes damping faster than it removes this drive.
\end{enumerate}

With the klystron measured in normal operation (\(g_A = 0.40\), \(k_\text{AP} = 0.10\), \cref{sec:robinson-open}) the third regime changes sign for the timing (0.84 at 0 dB) but hardly for the energy (1.13), and the first is unchanged. The resonant estimate \eqref{eq:rob-var} is within 1\% of the full integral at 0 dB and above, and a time-domain simulation with white noise injected at the klystron output reproduces the jitter without feedback, at 0 dB and at 6 dB to within 11\%. As a scale, a white klystron phase noise of \(10^{-11}\;\text{rad}^2/\text{Hz}\) (\(-113\) dBc/Hz) moves the beam by \(0.05^\circ\) rms at 0 dB.

\textbf{Setting the loop for low jitter.} The quantity to minimize at each current is the jitter that matters to users, subject to the damping limit \(\sigma \ge 2\alpha_\text{rad}\) of \cref{sec:robinson-procedure}. For the timing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-target}
\langle\Delta\phi^2\rangle \simeq \frac{\omega_s^4}{8\,\omega_\text{coh}^2\,\sigma}\sum_x \lvert h_x(j\omega_\text{coh})\rvert^2\,S_{u_x}(f_\text{coh}) + \sum_L \lvert\chi(j\omega_L)\rvert^2\,n_L^2 ;
\end{equation}
for the energy and the orbit, the first term with \(\omega_\text{coh}^2\) replaced by \((\alpha_c\omega_\text{RF})^2\), and without the second. Every factor depends on the gain, the loop phase and the integral path; the source spectra \(S_{u_x}\) are the unknowns. Because the three regimes pull the gain in opposite directions, the best setting depends on which sources dominate near \(f_\text{coh}\), which has not been measured (\cref{sec:robinson-open}). The integral path of the LLRF9 is the clearest case: its gain at the line harmonics lowers the second term, and its phase decides whether it also lowers \(\sigma\) and so raises the first (\cref{sec:robinson-int}).

Records of normal operation settle it (\cref{sec:robinson-open}): \(g_A = 0.40\) and \(k_\text{AP} \approx 0.1\). With these values, and with gain, delay and \(Q_L\) refitted at 20--22 mA with the same klystron, the model fits the high-current runs to 0.013--0.018 and places the cavities within 4 kHz of the power-balance detuning. As a cross-check, transfer curves measured in 2013 on a tube since replaced give \(d\ln P_\text{out}/d\ln P_\text{drive} = 0.2\)--0.45 near the top of the curves at 72--80 kV with 330--360 mA of beam \R{42}.
\item \textbf{The Robinson mode.} Near \(\pm f_s\) the high-current records, analyzed with 78 Hz bins, show the beam's coherent mode as a pole pair: 9.38 and 9.42 kHz with \(\sigma \approx 1.0\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 363 and 395 mA, and 8.32--8.44 kHz with \(\sigma = (0.97\)--\(1.46)\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 494--497 mA; the run of 10 November 2025 gives 8.65 kHz and \(1.6\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 485 mA. The damping is close to that of the synchrotron line of 8 October below; the frequency at 494--497 mA is 5\% lower. The gain setting accounts for little of that: \texttt{COUNTS.A} was 0.9 dB on 2 October and 1.3 dB on 8 October, which in the model moves the frequency by only 50 Hz, a tenth of the difference. The klystron's small-signal response, which shifts the frequency by hundreds of hertz (\cref{sec:robinson-open}), and the noise being injected on 2 October are the candidates.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The synchrotron line today.} Two captures of the cavity-A probe spectrum on 8 October 2026 \R{40}, each fitted with one Lorentzian per sideband and paired with a snapshot taken within three minutes, give the line of \cref{tab:robinson-today}. Its frequency has hardly changed since 2021, but its damping has more than halved: \(\sigma = (1.09\)--\(1.16)\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\), 2.5--2.7 times radiation damping. The proportional model is now 60--70\% too high. An integral path that shares the loop phase lowers the damping (\cref{tab:robinson-integral}); with \(f_i = 1\) kHz it reproduces the damping of both captures within 7\% and the frequency within 2\%. This value is an effective one. The corner trades against the loop-phase error: 0.8--1.0 kHz for \(\theta\) between \(-5^\circ\), the value fitted at 497 mA (\cref{tab:mdc-fits}), and 0. It also depends on how the loop behaves at high current: the effective parameters fitted at 497 mA without the integral path (\(G = 0.43\), \(Q_L = 4800\), 680 ns) reproduce the frequency as well (8.82--8.84 kHz) and need \(f_i = 1.5\)--1.8 kHz. The network actually installed, the lag network (\cref{sec:loop-direct}), corresponds to 2.9 kHz with a 0.26 kHz leak; with it the model gives \(\sigma \approx -390\;\text{s}^{-1}\) with the loop of \cref{tab:robinson-model} and \(460\;\text{s}^{-1}\), barely above radiation damping, with the effective loop. The lead network, worth about 2\%, is left out. Because an integral path can only lower the damping in this model, the 2021 line, 35\% above the proportional model, suggests that the integral path was then off or weaker.

\textbf{What the line says about the beam's motion.} The same captures give the size of the motion, not only its damping. Each sideband of the line carries the fraction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-line-area}
\frac{P_\pm}{P_\text{c}} = \frac{I_{b1}^2\,\lvert Z_{b,A}(\pm j\omega_\text{coh})\rvert^2}{2\,V_A^2}\,\langle\Delta\phi^2\rangle
\end{equation}
of the carrier power, where \(Z_{b,A} = Z_A/(1 + G_\text{OL})\) is the closed-loop impedance of the probed cavity and \(V_A\) its voltage. The capture of 15:35, taken with the RMS detector, shows that broadband noise is not the main drive. A comb of 60 Hz harmonics runs through the line, strongest on the 6-pulse family of D5 (\cref{sec:d5}): the harmonics at 8640 and 9000 Hz, which flank \(f_\text{coh}\), stand 16 dB above the noise beside them at \(-57\) and \(-55\) dBc. With the harmonics masked, the broadband line carries \(-58.5\) and \(-59.8\) dBc on the two sidebands. Two models reproduce the measured damping: the effective integral path of \cref{tab:robinson-today}, with \(\lvert Z_{b,A}\rvert = 343\) and \(357\;\text{k}\Omega\) at the two sidebands, and the lag network with the klystron measured in normal operation (\cref{sec:robinson-open}), for which the two-quadrature form of Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq} gives the corresponding factor. From the broadband line they put the beam's phase motion at \(0.19^\circ\) and \(0.14^\circ\) rms, and by \cref{eq:rob-var} the drive at \(S_n(f_\text{coh}) = 1.8\) and \(1.1\times10^{-11}\;\text{rad}^2/\text{Hz}\). Each harmonic is the coherent sum of a field modulation and the beam's response to it; for the harmonics at 8640 and 9000 Hz the models put the beam's share of the power at 0.8--1.3, whether the HVPS modulates the klystron's amplitude or its phase. The harmonics that the beam dominates add \(0.55^\circ\) and \(0.36^\circ\), 93--99\% of it from within 1 kHz of \(f_\text{coh}\). In total the beam's phase moves by \(0.58^\circ\) and \(0.39^\circ\) rms (3.4 and 2.3 ps), four to six times the field-phase specification of \cref{sec:field-stability}, and its energy by \(\Delta E/E_0 = 1.6\) and \(1.0\times10^{-4}\) rms \eqref{eq:rob-energy}. The probe-signal noise beside the line, \(-106\) and \(-103\) dBc/Hz on the upper and the lower side, corresponds to \(4.1\times10^{-11}\;\text{rad}^2/\text{Hz}\) if the two sidebands are uncorrelated, 2.3--3.9 times the broadband drive: the floor is not the drive, because below \(f_\text{coh}\) it contains the beam's own response and at 30 Hz resolution the skirts of the comb raise it. Two observations remain open: the upper sideband is 1.3 dB stronger than the lower one, where both models predict 0.35 dB weaker, and the passive spectrum shows neither which source drives the broadband line nor whether the harmonics are amplitude or phase modulation (\cref{sec:robinson-open}).

The line harmonics between 60 and 1080 Hz, measured in the same capture with a 3 Hz resolution, carry \(-51.5\) dBc in total, mostly at 60 and 720 Hz. The beam follows them (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}): they add \(0.15^\circ\) of timing jitter if they are phase modulation and \(0.06^\circ\) if they are amplitude modulation, but almost nothing to the energy. With the motion near \(f_\text{coh}\) they give \(0.40\)--\(0.60^\circ\) (2.3--3.5 ps) of timing jitter. Since 2021 the damping has fallen from 2.5 to \(1.1\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\); for the same drive, that alone has raised the variance of the broadband motion 2.3-fold, and the variance driven by the harmonics at 9000 and 8640 Hz 2.2--2.9-fold and 3.8--4.4-fold.

\textbf{Findings.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Robinson margin is adequate, but smaller than in 2021}: 2.5--2.7 times radiation damping today (\(Q = 24\)--26, within the acceptance limit \(Q \le 35\) of \cref{sec:robinson-procedure}), against six times in 2021. In the model, the integral path at its effective 1 kHz costs about 40\% of the damping that the proportional loop would leave. The present low gain is a sound choice for SPEAR3; raising it would buy impedance reduction that no instability requires and cost damping.
\item \textbf{The model does not reproduce the damping with the installed integral network.} Three measurements agree with the lag network of the RFgui loop models (\(f_i = 2.9\) kHz, leak 0.26 kHz): with it in the model the low-current closed-loop fits return the \(Q_L\) of the reflection and open-loop data; fitted within \(\pm 25\) kHz of the carrier, the same records give \(f_i = 3.0\)--4.2 kHz; and in records of 2 June 2020 the ratio of drive modulation to cavity error at the HVPS lines between 0.36 and 2.9 kHz follows the network's shape, with \(G \approx 2\) at the setting of the time \R{42}, and excludes a proportional controller. With that network the linear model leaves the beam unstable at 500 mA with the loop of \cref{tab:robinson-model} and barely damped with the effective loop, whereas the synchrotron line and the closed-loop records show \(\sigma = (1.0\)--\(1.6)\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) at 363--497 mA. The \(f_i \approx 1\) kHz of \cref{tab:robinson-today} is therefore an effective value, and the model misses about \(10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) of damping; the klystron's amplitude compression, measured in normal operation, supplies most of it (\cref{sec:robinson-open}). A synchrotron line with \texttt{INTCOMP} off at reduced current would measure the integral path's real cost: at 300 mA the model predicts \(\sigma = 1.10\times10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) (\(Q = 27\)) without the integral path, \(790\;\text{s}^{-1}\) (\(Q = 38\)) with the effective 1 kHz path and \(180\;\text{s}^{-1}\) (\(Q = 168\)) with the lag network.
\item \textbf{Frequency-offset tracking is on}, although \R{12} reserves it for diagnostics. It rotates the loop phase by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rob-track}
\phi_\text{tr} = -360^\circ\,F_\text{tr}\,\tau_d\,\overline{\Delta f},
\end{equation}
with \(F_\text{tr} = 0.8\) (\texttt{PHASE.F}), \(\tau_d = 700\) ns (\texttt{PHASE.B}, \cref{sec:delay-budget}) and \(\overline{\Delta f}\) the mean of the IOC's tuner-model estimates \texttt{FREQ:\allowbreak{}OFFS}. Those estimates disagree with the detuning implied by the measured reflection and power balance --- by more than 60 kHz for cavity 3 --- so \(\phi_\text{tr} = +11.6^\circ\), against \(+7.2^\circ\) from the physical detuning. A positive \(\phi_\text{tr}\) is a lag, because a more negative setting leads (\cref{tab:mdc-fits}). The excess, which drifted between \(+3^\circ\) and \(+6^\circ\) on 8 October, therefore lowers the damping by 5--11\%, and the loop phase follows a stale calibration rather than the cavities. Either switch tracking off and re-trim \texttt{PHASE.C} by the \(\phi_\text{tr}\) then in use, or recalibrate the tuner model (Make Polynomials, \cref{eq:tuner-poly}). If tracking stays on, \texttt{PHASE.B} should also be set to the present delay, 625 ns: its 699.5 ns most likely dates from the klystron replaced in 2020 (\cref{sec:delay-budget}) and makes \(\phi_\text{tr}\) 12\% too large.
\item \textbf{The klystron runs at 99\% of the IOC's 800 kW forward-power limit}, with the HVPS loop holding the drive 4.4 dB below the saturation setting.
\item \textbf{The beam's phase moves by \(0.39\)--\(0.58^\circ\) rms near \(f_\text{coh}\)} (2.3--3.4 ps), four to six times the field-phase specification. Most of it is driven by the 6-pulse HVPS harmonics at 8640 and 9000 Hz, which flank \(f_\text{coh}\) above the reach of the ripple loop; broadband noise adds \(0.14\)--\(0.19^\circ\). As the current rises, \(f_\text{coh}\) sweeps through this comb, and both models that reproduce the damping put a crossing of a 360 Hz harmonic at 220--230 mA, a candidate for the larger oscillation seen near 200 mA. Which source sets the broadband drive --- the klystron, the measurement or the reference --- and whether the harmonics modulate amplitude or phase decide which remedy works (\cref{sec:robinson-noise}); neither has been measured. The direct-loop study (\texttt{Studies/optLLRF}) plans the measurements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Setting the Direct Loop: Procedure and Acceptance}\label{sec:robinson-procedure}

The procedure applies to the legacy station now and to the LLRF9 at commissioning; steps that differ are marked. Steps 1--3 need no beam or only low current; steps 4 and 5 are machine-development work at operating current.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Preconditions.} Tuners converged (load-angle errors below \(1^\circ\)), reflected power at the coupler floor of \cref{eq:gamma-min} (1--3\%), klystron at least 10\% below saturation, gain tracking active. Record a snapshot of the quantities in \cref{tab:legacy-live}.
\item \textbf{Delay and phase} (no beam or below 50 mA, cavities near \(f_\text{RF}\)). Measure the loop response --- on the legacy system with RFgui Measure Direct Closed (closed loop, beam undisturbed) or RFguiInvasive Configure Direct (open loop; it also sets the phase and gain and writes the fitted delay to \texttt{PHASE.B}) \R{41}, on the LLRF9 with its network analyzer --- and fit gain, \(Q\), delay and the phase at resonance. Set the phase so that the fitted phase at resonance is \(180^\circ\) within \(\pm 5^\circ\) (\(\theta = 0\)). On the legacy system a more negative \texttt{PHASE.C} is a lead (\cref{tab:mdc-fits}); on the LLRF9, establish the sign once by stepping the phase setting by \(+10^\circ\). A lag is the dangerous direction (\cref{fig:robinson-phase}).
\item \textbf{Gain.} Choose \(G\) from \cref{fig:robinson-gain}, which is for 500 mA; for a different maximum current, recompute it with the model of \cref{sec:robinson-model}. At 500 mA, \(G \le 3\) dB keeps \(\sigma \ge 2\alpha_\text{rad}\) for phase errors within \(\pm 15^\circ\) (\(G \le 2\) dB for \(\pm 30^\circ\)) and leaves more than 13 dB of margin to the delay-limited instability (\cref{tab:robinson-delay}). The present 0 dB lies inside this range. Within it the gain also sets how the noise of the RF system moves the beam (\cref{fig:robinson-noise}): lower gain is better against measurement, reference and klystron phase noise, higher gain only against klystron amplitude noise. Do not raise the gain for field regulation alone; until the sources are measured (\cref{sec:robinson-open}), stay near the present setting.
\item \textbf{Integral path.} Legacy: the network is fixed; measure its effect on the damping by comparing the synchrotron line with \texttt{INTCOMP} on and off at reduced current. LLRF9: start with \(f_i \le 1\) kHz, set \(\Delta\theta_i\) by rotating it until the line is most strongly damped, and raise \(f_i\) only as far as the line-harmonic rejection requires (\cref{tab:robinson-integral}). Judge the setting by the beam's jitter \eqref{eq:rob-target} as well as by its damping.
\item \textbf{Verify with beam} (500 mA). Record a cavity-probe spectrum (20 kHz span, 30 Hz RBW, RMS detector, about 100 power-averaged sweeps, which take 15 s) and fit a Lorentzian to each synchrotron sideband \R{40}. Accept if \(Q \le 35\) (\(\sigma \ge 2\alpha_\text{rad}\)) and the frequency lies within 5\% of the model run on a snapshot taken at the same time; record the 360 and 720 Hz lines, and the power in each sideband of the synchrotron line, which gives the beam's phase motion \eqref{eq:rob-line-area}.
\item \textbf{Re-verify} after any change of loop gain or phase, klystron, HVPS, drive amplifier, cables or couplers, and when the maximum current changes by more than 20\%.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warnbox}
\textbf{Warning signs}: at 500 mA, a synchrotron line with \(Q \gtrsim 40\), or a coherent frequency close to the incoherent 10.0 kHz (too much gain, or a lag error). At lower current both are normal --- the model gives \(Q \approx 50\) and 9.8 kHz at 200 mA --- so compare with the model at the measured current. Also: growing or splitting sidebands (Robinson anti-damping, often from the integral path); 360 or 720 Hz lines rising by more than 10 dB (ripple loop off or mis-set); a synchrotron line whose power rises by more than 3 dB at unchanged width (a stronger drive, D10); klystron drive approaching the saturation setting (no headroom left for the loop).
\end{warnbox}

\subsection{Model Limits and Open Questions}\label{sec:robinson-open}

The model assumes rigid bunches, a uniform fill and equal vector-sum weights, and it treats the klystron as linear. Rigid bunches describe only the dipole motion: the quadrupole (bunch-length) mode, which amplitude noise near \(2f_s\) drives, lies outside the model. For the dipole mode at the present operating point the first three assumptions do not matter. The last may: the closed-loop response at 363--497 mA does not follow the linear model (\cref{tab:mdc-fits}), and with the installed integral network the model lacks about \(10^3\;\text{s}^{-1}\) of damping at high current (\cref{sec:robinson-int}). A klystron that responds differently to amplitude and phase perturbations makes the loop act differently on the two quadratures, which a loop described by one complex gain cannot represent and the two-quadrature form of Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq} can. The closed-loop records of the present tube at 363--497 mA require such a klystron (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}); as cross-checks, two data sets from tubes since replaced show the same behavior: the transfer curves of 2013 flatten near saturation, and in records of 2 June 2020, taken at operating power, switching on the direct loop reduced the 360 and 720 Hz phase lines of the cavity field by 35 and 29 dB but the amplitude lines by only 6 and 2 dB \R{42}.

The asymmetry also changes the Robinson mode. A time-domain version of the model, which agrees with the characteristic equation to within 0.3\% for a linear klystron, includes the klystron's \(g_A\) and \(k_\text{AP}\). At 497 mA, with the lag network and \(G = 0.875\), \(g_A = 0.75\) raises \(\sigma\) from \(-270\) to \(+140\;\text{s}^{-1}\) and \(k_\text{AP} = 0.6\) raises it to \(+340\;\text{s}^{-1}\), while each lowers the frequency by 300--480 Hz. Klystron asymmetry can therefore supply the missing damping. The two-quadrature characteristic equation of Appendix~\ref{app:char-eq} reproduces these values, and those below, to 1\%.

Records of normal operation measure both parameters without disturbing the beam. At each trip the station freezes, together, the drive I/Q (RFP signal memory), the probe I/Q of one cavity and the klystron output amplitude (IQ\&A history) \R{42}. In the 4.9 ms of normal operation before the trip, ten such records give \(g_A = 0.40 \pm 0.05\) from the output amplitude against the drive (coherence 0.93--0.98 at 50--200 kHz, above the HVPS lines), the same at 40\% and at full output power, consistent with the HVPS loop holding the drive at a fixed point of the transfer curve. The probe's response to the drive amplitude, relative to its response to the drive phase, gives \(k_\text{AP} = 0.10 \pm 0.04\) whatever the cavity and beam; this estimate leans slightly towards a linear klystron (it returns \(g_A = 0.50\)). With \(g_A = 0.40\) the model reaches the measured damping --- \(\sigma = 950\;\text{s}^{-1}\) for a phase-path gain of 1.0, \(450\;\text{s}^{-1}\) for 1.3 --- but at 7.9--8.2 kHz, 5--10\% below the measured frequency. Amplitude compression in the klystron is therefore the main source of the missing damping. Open are the frequency offset and the phase-path gain: since \(g_A \approx 0.4\) holds at low current too, the \(G = 0.88\) of \cref{tab:mdc-fits} is the gain that a one-dimensional injection sees, and the refits with \(g_A = 0.40\) place the phase-path gain between 1.0 and 1.4 without fixing it. Seven measurements would remove the remaining uncertainties:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the synchrotron line at reduced current with \texttt{INTCOMP} and \texttt{LEADCOMP} switched off in turn, which measures the effect of each network on the damping directly;
\item the closed-loop measurement at low current and near 500 mA with independent I and Q noise, or with I-only and Q-only noise in turn, to separate the responses to amplitude and phase perturbations, together with the loop settings at the time (\texttt{PHASE.L}, \texttt{COUNTS.L});
\item the amplitude and phase transfer curves (AM/AM and AM/PM) of the present klystron at the operating voltage and beam current, to confirm \(g_A\) and \(k_\text{AP}\) from the fault records and give their dependence on drive level and voltage, together with records that hold the klystron's output phase;
\item the synchrotron line at two or three currents (for example 100, 300 and 500 mA) with the loop settings recorded, to fit the effective loop phase directly;
\item the LLRF9 total loop delay, measured with its own network analyzer;
\item the noise spectra of the klystron output, of the cavity probes and of the reference near \(f_s\), without beam or at low current and with the loop settings recorded, which give the spectral density of each source class of \cref{eq:rob-drive} near \(f_\text{coh}\);
\item the synchrotron line at operating current while a small noise band around \(f_\text{coh}\) is added to the drive, first in amplitude only and then in phase only, which measures the drive transfer of the klystron sources and, with the previous item, the gain and phase that minimize the beam's jitter \eqref{eq:rob-target}; the same records should explain why the upper sideband of the line is 1.3 dB stronger than the lower one (\cref{sec:robinson-legacy}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Direct loop calibration} & Direct, SummingNodeI/Q, GainStageI/Q & Characterizes direct loop gain stages and summing node offsets. I and Q channels measured independently to resolve cross-coupling \\
\textbf{Klystron chain} & ZeroKlysMults, DiffNodeOffsets, KlysStage, CompStage, CombStage, KlysDemod & Calibrates klystron forward path: drive modulator offsets, klystron gain, compensator stages, demodulator phase alignment \\
\textbf{Tuner \& modulator} & TuneStage, NullModulator & Calibrates tune-mode drive path; nulls RF modulator output (zero DAC → zero RF) \\
\textbf{Completion} & Finish/Done or Abort/Abend & Restores operational configuration, reports success/failure \\
\end{longtable*}

Each state includes retry logic (\texttt{NUM\_TRIES} iterations), severity checking on PV readbacks, and abort handling. The entire sequence takes \textasciitilde2--5 minutes.

\subsubsection{Drive Amplitude Calibration}\label{sec:cal-drive}


Establishes the mapping from DAC counts to klystron input drive power. Data files: \texttt{driveAmpCalibration.\allowbreak{}xlsx}, \texttt{klystronCouplerDriveAmpCalibrations.\allowbreak{}xlsx}.

The key relationship is the \textbf{drive conversion constant} \(D_\text{conv}\) (counts/kV), computed by \texttt{subIQampl2conv}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:dconv}
D_\text{conv} = \frac{A_\text{ref}}{V_\text{gap} \cdot (1 + G_\text{loop})}
\end{equation}
where \(A_\text{ref}\) is the reference amplitude (counts), \(V_\text{gap}\) is the measured gap voltage (kV), and \(G_\text{loop}\) is the feedback loop gain. This constant is used by the DAC loop \eqref{eq:dac-law} and recomputed whenever the operating point changes.

\subsubsection{Power Measurement Calibration}\label{sec:cal-power}


Maps RF detector voltages to physical power levels. Data files: \texttt{reflectedPowerCalibrations.\allowbreak{}xlsx}, \texttt{pulsarCouplerCalibration2049.\allowbreak{}xlsx}.

The \textbf{RF detector conversion loss} measured during calibration (\texttt{subIQampl2loss}):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:det-loss}
L_\text{det} = 20 \cdot \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{E_\text{conv} \cdot \sqrt{P_\text{cal}}}{A_\text{det}}\right) \;\text{dB}
\end{equation}
where \(E_\text{conv} = 0.31623\) (power conversion constant), \(P_\text{cal}\) is calibration power (mW), and \(A_\text{det}\) is measured detector amplitude (V). Accounts for cable attenuation, coupler directivity, and detector nonlinearity.

The \textbf{coupling factor} (VSWR-based, \texttt{subIQamplCplg}):

\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-meas}
\beta_\text{meas} = \frac{1 + r}{1 - r}, \qquad r = \frac{A_\text{refl}}{A_\text{fwd}}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Frequency and Detuning Calibration}\label{sec:cal-freq}


Maps tuner motor position to cavity resonant frequency offset. The polynomial model \eqref{eq:tuner-poly} is fitted from measured data: the tuner is stepped across its range and the resonant frequency measured at each position. Calibration coefficients \(p_0, \ldots, p_3\) and the voltage coefficient \(t_1\) are stored in EPICS PVs.

\subsubsection{Tuner Mode DAC Calibration}\label{sec:cal-tunemode}


Data file: \texttt{tuneModeDacCalibration.\allowbreak{}xlsx}. Establishes DAC counts-to-drive-power for the \texttt{STATION\_TUNE} state (low-power cavity conditioning). Separate from operational drive calibration (\cref{sec:cal-drive}) because the operating point differs.

\subsubsection{Signal Routing and Patch Panel}\label{sec:cal-routing}


Data file: \texttt{b132R11PatchPanel.\allowbreak{}xlsx}. Documents physical signal routing in Building 132 R11: cable lengths, attenuation values, connector types, and signal naming. Essential for verifying the delay budget (\cref{sec:delay-budget}) and signal levels.

\begin{keynote}
\textbf{Upgrade note}: Many calibrations will need adaptation for the LLRF9 system (different ADC/DAC ranges, signal levels, processing gains). The polynomial frequency model \eqref{eq:tuner-poly} and combiner matrix calibration are expected to transfer; drive and power detector calibrations require new measurements.
\end{keynote}

\section{Derivation of the Characteristic Equation}\label{app:char-eq}

This appendix derives \cref{eq:rob-char} for rigid point bunches in a uniform fill of \(M\) equally spaced bunches, with a narrowband impedance and a linear klystron, and then generalizes it to an external drive and to a klystron that treats amplitude and phase differently.

\textbf{Motion of one bunch.} Let \(\Delta\phi\) be the phase of a bunch behind the synchronous phase, in RF radians (positive = late), and \(\Delta E\) its energy deviation. Per turn the bunch gains \(e[V_\text{RF}\cos(\phi_s + \Delta\phi) + \delta V] - U_0\), where \(\delta V\) is the change of beam-induced voltage it meets; above transition a higher energy makes it arrive later, \(d\Delta\phi/dt = \alpha_c\omega_\text{RF}\,\Delta E/E_0\). To first order, with radiation damping added \R{8},

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-motion}
\frac{d^2\Delta\phi}{dt^2} + 2\alpha_\text{rad}\,\frac{d\Delta\phi}{dt} + \omega_s^2\,\Delta\phi = \frac{\alpha_c\,\omega_\text{RF}}{(E_0/e)\,T_\text{rev}}\,\delta V,
\end{equation}
with \(\omega_s^2\) and \(\alpha_\text{rad}\) as in \cref{eq:rob-coeffs}.

\textbf{Beam current.} Written as an envelope on the carrier \(e^{j\omega_\text{RF}t}\), like the signals of \cref{sec:iq}, the fundamental beam current of the unperturbed train is \(I_{b1}e^{-j\phi_s}\), with \(I_{b1} = 2I_b\). A bunch that is late by \(\Delta\phi\) multiplies its contribution by \(e^{-j\Delta\phi} \approx 1 - j\Delta\phi\), so the motion changes the current envelope by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-current}
\delta\vec{I}_b = -j\,I_{b1}\,e^{-j\phi_s}\,\Delta\phi .
\end{equation}

\textbf{Coupled-bunch mode \(n\).} In mode \(n\) the phase advances by \(2\pi n/M\) from one bunch to the next, \(\Delta\phi_k(t) = \text{Re}\bigl[\Phi\,e^{st}e^{j2\pi nk/M}\bigr]\). Bunch \(k\) passes the cavities at \(t_k = kT_\text{rev}/M\) plus whole turns, where \(e^{j2\pi nk/M} = e^{jn\omega_\text{rev}t_k}\), so the bunches sample the single signal \(\Delta\phi(t) = \text{Re}\bigl[\Phi\,e^{s't}\bigr]\) with \(s' = s + jn\omega_\text{rev}\). Its aliases lie multiples of \(Mf_\text{rev}\) away, far outside the cavity resonance, and are dropped.

\textbf{Induced voltage.} The beam drives the cavities through the closed-loop impedance of \cref{eq:rob-zb}, \(\delta\vec{V} = -Z_b\,\delta\vec{I}_b\), and a bunch at the synchronous phase meets \(\delta V = \text{Re}\bigl[\delta\vec{V}e^{j\phi_s}\bigr]\). Because \(\Delta\phi\) is real, \(\Delta\phi = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\Phi e^{s't} + \bar\Phi e^{\bar s' t}\bigr)\), and each exponential passes through \(Z_b\) at its own complex frequency. Collecting the terms in \(e^{s't}\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-dv}
\delta V = \text{Re}\Bigl[\tfrac{j}{2}\,I_{b1}\,\Phi\,\bigl(Z_b(s') - \overline{Z_b(\bar s')}\bigr)\,e^{s't}\Bigr].
\end{equation}

\textbf{Result.} Substituting \cref{eq:app-dv} into \cref{eq:app-motion} for bunch \(k\) and equating the coefficients of \(e^{st}e^{j2\pi nk/M}\) gives \(s^2 + 2\alpha_\text{rad}s + \omega_s^2 = j\kappa\,[Z_b(s') - \overline{Z_b(\bar s')}]\) with \(\kappa = \alpha_c\omega_\text{RF}I_{b1}/(2(E_0/e)T_\text{rev})\), which is \cref{eq:rob-char,eq:rob-coeffs}. The two terms are the responses at the upper and the lower synchrotron sideband: for \(s \approx j\omega_s\) and \(n = 0\) they are \(Z_b(j\omega_s)\) and \(\overline{Z_b(-j\omega_s)}\), which leads to \cref{eq:rob-first} and to the Robinson criterion \eqref{eq:robinson-crit}. Without feedback and with identical cavities, multiplying \(D_0(s)\) by the cavity denominator \((s + \omega_{1/2})^2 + \Delta\omega^2\) gives Pedersen's quartic \R{35} with radiation damping added; at low current the first-order rate is that of Robinson \R{14} and Wilson \R{10}.

\textbf{External drive.} An RF error that does not come from the motion adds to \(\delta V\) in \cref{eq:app-motion} the voltage \(\text{Re}\bigl[\delta\vec{V}_\text{ext}\,e^{j\phi_s}\bigr]\), where \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{ext}\) is the change of the total gap envelope that it causes with the bunches held fixed. An error common to all bunches drives only \(n = 0\), and the result becomes \(D_0(s)\,\Delta\phi = \omega_s^2\,n_\text{eff}\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-drive}
n_\text{eff} = \frac{\text{Re}\bigl[\delta\vec{V}_\text{ext}\,e^{j\phi_s}\bigr]}{V_\text{RF}\sin\phi_s},
\end{equation}
which is \(\varepsilon_A\cot\phi_s - \varepsilon_\phi\) for \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{ext} = V_\text{RF}(\varepsilon_A + j\varepsilon_\phi)\) (\cref{eq:rob-driven}).

\textbf{Two quadratures.} A klystron near saturation responds differently to amplitude and phase (\cref{sec:robinson-open}), and the bunch senses one combination of the two; neither fits a loop described by one complex gain. Write every envelope as the real vector \((I, Q)^\mathsf{T}\) of its components and every complex-coefficient transfer function \(F(s)\) as the real matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-real}
\mathbf{F}(s) = \begin{pmatrix} F_r & -F_i \\ F_i & F_r \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F_r = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[F(s) + \bar F(s)\bigr],\quad
F_i = \tfrac{1}{2j}\bigl[F(s) - \bar F(s)\bigr],\quad
\bar F(s) = \overline{F(\bar s)},
\end{equation}
whose entries are the transforms of the real and the imaginary part of the impulse response. The klystron becomes \(\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{R}(\theta_g)\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} g_A & 0 \\ k_\text{AP} & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\mathbf{R}(-\theta_g)\), with \(\mathbf{R}(\theta)\) a rotation by \(\theta\) and \(\theta_g\) the phase of the generator current; \(g_A = 1\), \(k_\text{AP} = 0\) is a linear klystron. With \(\mathbf{P}_\Sigma = \sum_k \mathbf{Z}_k\), \(\mathbf{P}_\text{m} = \sum_k w_k\mathbf{Z}_k\) and the forward path \(\mathbf{F}_\text{fw}(s) = \mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{C}_\text{fw}(s)/R_L\), where \(C_\text{fw}(s) = C(s)\,\omega_a e^{-s\tau_d}/(s + \omega_a)\) is everything in \cref{eq:rob-loop} but the cavities, the closed-loop impedance seen by the beam is \(\mathbf{Z}_b = \mathbf{P}_\Sigma\bigl(\mathbf{1} + \mathbf{F}_\text{fw}\mathbf{P}_\text{m}\bigr)^{-1}\). The motion changes the beam current along \(\hat b = (-\sin\phi_s, -\cos\phi_s)^\mathsf{T}\) \eqref{eq:app-current}, and the bunch senses the voltage along \(\hat p = (\cos\phi_s, -\sin\phi_s)^\mathsf{T}\) \eqref{eq:app-drive}, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-iq}
\begin{gathered}
D_0(s) = s^2 + 2\alpha_\text{rad}\,s + \omega_s^2 + 2\kappa\,\hat p^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Z}_b(s)\,\hat b,\\
n_\text{eff}(s) = \frac{\hat p^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Z}_b(s)\bigl[\delta\vec{I}_\text{kly} - \mathbf{F}_\text{fw}(s)\,(\delta\vec{V}_\text{m} - \delta\vec{V}_\text{ref})\bigr]}{V_\text{RF}\sin\phi_s},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where \(\delta\vec{I}_\text{kly}\) is noise at the klystron output (noise ahead of the klystron enters as \(\mathbf{K}\) times its own vector) and \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{m}\), \(\delta\vec{V}_\text{ref}\) are as in \cref{eq:rejection}. The coefficient of each source is its transfer function \(h_x\) of \cref{eq:rob-drive}. For a linear klystron \(\hat p^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Z}_b\hat b = -\tfrac{j}{2}\bigl[Z_b(s) - \overline{Z_b(\bar s)}\bigr]\), and the first line is \cref{eq:rob-char} for \(n = 0\) to rounding error; for a symmetric loop (cavities on tune, \(\theta = 0\)) each \(h_x\) is the open-loop path divided by \(1 + G_\text{OL}\), or multiplied by \(T\). With the klystron of \cref{sec:robinson-open} the roots agree with the time-domain simulation to 1\%; with a linear klystron, white noise injected at the klystron output of that simulation reproduces the beam jitter of \cref{fig:robinson-noise} without feedback, at 0 dB and at 6 dB to within 11\%. Two static checks are exact: without feedback a phase step of the klystron moves the beam by the same angle, and with an integral path a phase step of the reference does.
